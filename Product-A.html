A count is only meaningful once you know the thing counted, over what period and area, with what exclusions. An assault, a nonviolent protest, a stopped plot, and a statement praising violence are not interchangeable. Political parties, movements, and categories assigned by researchers also describe different things.

What counts as an event?

One source might ask how many public gatherings were recorded. Another might ask how many planned attacks reached a defined threshold. Each answers a different question. The four examples below describe fictional collections, not publishers or real coverage.

Gathering log
Counts documented gatherings, including peaceful ones. A threatening message alone is outside this fictional definition.
Attack record
Counts completed attacks that meet its written criteria. Stopped plans are outside this fictional definition.
Plot review
Records planned attacks meeting its evidence threshold, even if no one was harmed. A rumor alone would not qualify.
Harm register
Counts people harmed rather than events. One event can contribute several people.

Takeaway: A source can be useful without measuring everything. Check its unit and rules before comparing numbers.

Who acted, why, and whom did they target?

Fictional example: A person attacks a public official outside a meeting. The occurrence is documented, but there is no reliable evidence about motive.

Actor affiliation
Record supported evidence about the actor separately.
Event motive
Unknown in this example. An affiliation alone does not settle why this act happened.
Target identity
The target is a public official. That fact does not establish the actor’s affiliation or motive.

Takeaway: Changing the official’s party would not change the evidence about motive. Likewise, one participant’s actions do not establish what every person at a gathering did.

Takeaway: Choose the metric that answers your question. Check the geography, starting and ending dates, and whether a recent period is complete.

What does a blank or unresolved entry mean?

Observed zero
The source covered the period and coded this category, and recorded no qualifying events. That is not proof no event occurred.
Not covered
The period or place was outside the source’s collection scope.
Not coded
The source did not record the category or field being asked about.
Unknown
Evidence is insufficient to assign a value, such as a motive.
Contested
Credible accounts disagree on a value. Preserve the disagreement rather than converting it to certainty.

Takeaway: Preserve the reason a value is missing or unresolved. Do not replace these states with zero.

How should a claim change as evidence changes?

Fictional chronology:

  1. A report describes an incident, but does not settle who acted.
  2. A subsequent record alleges that a person committed an offense. Describe it as an allegation, not a conclusion.
  3. Later records give a disposition. Describe only the outcome the source supports, with its date.
  4. A later correction changes one detail. Identify the change without treating every earlier or remaining claim as resolved.

Takeaway: Assess occurrence, identity, motive, and record status separately. Keep the history visible. This is a reading example, not a legal guide.

A collection of striking quotations can illustrate how framing works, but cannot establish how common a type of rhetoric is. That requires a defined sample, comparable speakers and opportunities, and rules to distinguish quotation from endorsement.

Before you repeat a comparison

Ask which release, dates, places and definitions it uses. Check whether it counts events or people; whether it includes plots, demonstrations, or state actions; and how it handles unknowns. A difference supported within that frame can be reported without making a claim about all members of a party or movement. Do not force agreement between unlike measures.
