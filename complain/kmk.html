A reported harm, a disputed remedy

K.M.K. and girls’ wrestling — a Complain or Constrain? case study

Dated snapshot: 2 November 2026. The Supreme Court emergency application was still pending when checked. This is an educational example, not live coverage or legal advice. Consult the official docket for later developments.

A person can report a serious injury and still face legitimate questions about the remedy they request. Examining that remedy does not require deciding that the person is dishonest. Equally, taking a report seriously does not establish that every restriction proposed in response is necessary.

The reported harm

K.M.K., a Washington high-school wrestler, reported that a transgender opponent sexually assaulted her during a December 2025 match. That is an allegation, not a finding made by this case study or a final judicial determination. In its October 13 opposition, Puyallup School District acknowledged her report of feeling hurt, frightened and violated. The district said it contacted law enforcement, retained an independent investigator, offered supportive measures and facilitated a school transfer. 1

The district also reported that prosecutors declined criminal charges and that its Title IX investigation remained open. Those are the positions and investigation status recorded in that filing; this page does not independently establish a later outcome. A decision not to charge does not establish that a report was false, and an open investigation does not establish that the allegation was proved. 1

What relief is being requested?

The emergency application asks for protection against matching K.M.K. with transgender girls in girls’ wrestling. It is important not to describe the immediate requested injunction as a blanket nationwide ban: the applicants frame the accommodation around K.M.K.’s participation. They also ask the Supreme Court to take up the underlying legal questions before the Ninth Circuit finishes its appeal. 2 3

Their October 15 reply sets out alternatives. Officials would avoid a matchup when they know or have reason to know that the opponent falls within the category the applicants contest. If the bracket nevertheless produces such a matchup, officials would withdraw K.M.K. or notify her mother so that she could withdraw. A forfeit would receive treatment no less favorable than the most favorable comparable exceptions in the rules. These are proposals by the applicants, not accommodations the courts have ordered. 3

The applicants argue that officials could implement the accommodation without disclosing another student’s identity or transgender status to the family. They also assert safety, equal-opportunity and parental-rights concerns independent of the reported assault. A fair analysis must address those arguments rather than reducing the entire lawsuit to one allegation. 3

Who else could be affected?

The respondents dispute the legal entitlement to emergency relief and the feasibility of the proposed arrangements. The district points to limited information about other students, control over tournament brackets and wrestling rules, and possible consequences for minors’ privacy. WIAA opposes both emergency intervention and immediate Supreme Court review. These are contested positions, not Supreme Court findings. 1 4

The distinction is between the named beneficiary and the practical effects of an order. A remedy for one student can still require decisions about a category of potential opponents. Conversely, that possibility does not by itself prove that an individualized accommodation is unworkable or unjustified. The alternatives in the October 15 reply deserve their own assessment, rather than being treated as identical to every earlier formulation.

What have the courts actually decided?

On September 25, the Ninth Circuit denied an injunction pending appeal. Its order addressed the demanding standard for emergency relief, the lack of demonstrated immediate urgency at that stage and the balance of hardships. It questioned whether the proposed protections extended beyond the reported incident involving one opponent and considered privacy and implementation concerns. The order did not finally decide the lawsuit or determine that the reported assault did or did not occur. 2

The Supreme Court docket, checked on November 2, listed the October 13 oppositions and October 15 reply but no order granting or denying the emergency application. Requesting responses was not a ruling for either side. The underlying legal claims remained unresolved in this emergency proceeding. 5

How to use this example fairly

Ask what evidence supports each requested protection, who would implement it, who else would be affected and whether a less restrictive approach could provide adequate protection. Distinguish a proposed alternative from one shown to work.

Do not treat one alleged incident as proof that an entire group is dangerous. Do not infer bad faith merely because a student seeks an accommodation. Do not dismiss a reported injury because the requested remedy may be overbroad. And do not assume that a remedy is proportionate simply because it names only one beneficiary.

This example illustrates a question, not a verdict about the student’s motives: how should an institution respond seriously to a reported harm while requiring a proposed restriction to be justified by evidence, legal authority and its effects on other people?
