# Timeline methods and source notes

[Read the guide](guide.md) · [Explore the U.S. timeline](timeline.md)

## Coverage and selection {#coverage}

This is a curated U.S. history of selected developments in rights, public institutions, and civil liberties, beginning in August 2019 and researched through **9 October 2026**. It is not a census of U.S. events, a national risk score, or a statement that unlisted issues are unimportant. Initial entries follow four questions: procurement versus services, a court ruling versus usable access, a new regulation versus an existing legal baseline, and an institution's powers versus its capacity to use them.

Selection favors developments with inspectable public documents, a meaningful before-and-after distinction, and a clear reason to revisit them. Linked events include restoration and subsequent loss of capacity. Topics do not receive equal numbers of entries for partisan balance. New entries may document older events discovered later; their event and first-recorded dates remain separate.

## What a source establishes {#sources}

Each event's expandable source notes name documents, locations within them, what they support, access limits, and check dates. A court opinion supports what the court held; an agency statement supports what the agency said or concluded. Neither independently proves every factual assertion it repeats. The Arizona opinion's staff summary expressly is not part of the opinion; entries use the judicial text and distinguish reported testimony from the holding.

The initial selection was researched with AI assistance, drafted, and challenged in a separate AI review. The owner directs this work; this is not independent legal certification, scholarly peer review, or testing with readers. The six questions are a teaching method, not a validated score. Public claims are traced to public documents rather than treated as verified because a research system saved them successfully.

### Initial source notes {#initial-notes}

- **NYC procurement:** City Record, 6 October 2026, printed p. 4241, Family and Child Health, EPIN 81627N0004. Its proposed dates do not establish commencement. The notice inconsistently lists a three-year term alongside a shorter date range, and two response times; those discrepancies remain visible. We do not certify an award, payment, or patient access from this notice.
- **Arizona birth certificates:** Ninth Circuit, *Roe v. Johnston*, Nos. 25-6970/25-6980, 28 April and 6 October 2026. The earlier stay is essential context. The later opinion, pp. 15–17, discusses barriers reported by plaintiffs and construes the court-order alternative. A current complete docket and mandate have not been verified. The entries therefore do not claim that a broader administrative route closed for the first time in October or that all amendments are banned.
- **Title IX:** court judgment and amendment in *Tennessee v. Cardona*, plus the 2026 Federal Register rule. The rule's effective date and codification changes need the rule itself; a political press release is insufficient. Source notes distinguish court text from the department's characterization of the case.
- **FEC capacity:** FEC announcements, annual reports, inspector-general reporting, and current leadership listing. Confirmation and taking office are distinct dates. The restoration entry is a temporary improvement, linked to subsequent losses. Loss of a quorum limits particular commission decisions; reporting duties and staff functions do not simply vanish.

Sources may be amended, moved, incomplete, or temporarily unavailable. A replacement URL alone does not establish that the document is unchanged. A substantive amendment requires comparison with the earlier version, a recorded reason, and editorial review. A paywall or failed retrieval is recorded as an access limit, never as proof that no later order exists.

## Dates, freshness, and uncertainty {#dates}

Every entry separates the event date and its precision, first recorded date, last substantive revision, last successful source check, and any established effective date. “Last checked” means the entry's required evidence and current-status questions were successfully examined; it is not a guarantee that nothing has happened since. Individual source checks can succeed while the overall entry remains incomplete.

A rebuild, successful HTTP response, or check of an unrelated topic does not refresh an entry. An uneventful successful check changes its check record and suggested review date, not its event date or last substantive revision. A failed or partial check retains the last successful date and states the gap. No recurring timeline check is active. Each entry's suggested manual review date flags when another comparison is needed; it does not promise that a check is scheduled or has happened. Review intervals remain visible, and an overdue entry stays overdue until actually rechecked. Without JavaScript these are explicit dates, not a claim of live monitoring. With JavaScript an age warning uses the reader's device date. No global “updated today” badge conceals old topics.

Legal or procedural status is prose, separate from action type and observed effects. A rule can exist in the published code while a court blocks it; an unadopted proposal can already affect behavior. Effects are labeled as documented, reported, measured, inferred, or forecast. A missing measurement does not establish no harm.

## Related events and corrections {#corrections}

Stable event links preserve the record as a history. Follow related-event links for later developments; older entries explain both the position at the time and the later context. A development adds to the chain. A correction fixes what our earlier account got wrong. Neither silently deletes the earlier account.

Substantive revisions preserve the preceding full entry in an append-only correction and revision history, with date, reason, and a link to the event. The timeline exposes that history beneath the entries. Its first corrections document changes to the publicly inspectable review draft before any site deployment. Earlier wording remains available with the reason it was corrected. Demonstration fixtures are tests, not news or fabricated public corrections.

[Report a correction](https://github.com/ExtiVaries/How-to-Hear-a-Bully/issues) with the stable event link, passage, and public evidence. GitHub requires an account and posts issues publicly. Do not post personal case details or identity documents. This route does not provide urgent help or guarantee a reply.

## Publication and reuse {#publication}

The repository holds one structured timeline source, from which the HTML, Markdown reading edition, and [public JSON](../../changes/timeline.json) are generated. The guide and these methods have canonical Markdown sources. Validation and review precede publication; failed updates keep the last good public release. Operational failures belong in the maintainer's task or CI failure report, with a short public coverage notice only if a prolonged interruption changes what readers can rely on.

This draft creates no recurring public writer. The initial workflow retains the existing research backend and prepares curated repository changes for review. Deployment remains a separate publication step after approval.

Reuse terms for this new guide, timeline, and methods have not been selected. Free access does not extend the original Bully guide's license to this material or to linked documents.
