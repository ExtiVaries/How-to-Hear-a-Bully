## Appeals court recognizes Arizona court-order route; rejects facial challenge {#us-az-birth-opinion-2026}

Event: 2026-10-06 (day precision). Action: ruling.

**What happened:** The Ninth Circuit rejected the facial constitutional challenge and interpreted the court-order route as available without surgery.

**Previously:** The broader injunction was already stayed in April, before its scheduled start.

**What changed:** The panel published its merits decision; the injunction was already stayed. This does not establish a new closure of a working simplified route.

**Scope:** Arizona-born people seeking sex-marker amendments, including those without surgery.

**Legal / procedural status:** Panel reverses summary judgment, vacates injunction, and remands; mandate not verified.

**What applies now:** The statute retains physician-verification and court-order routes. We have not verified current mandate, later orders, or registrar practice.

**Reported effect:** The opinion recounts mothers’ testimony that two applicants were denied orders because courts expected the registrar to require surgery. That is attributed testimony, not a national rate or a new post-ruling effect.

**Unresolved:** The court-order route does not establish easy access. Current costs, delays, and application outcomes remain unmeasured here.

## Title IX regulatory text catches up with the earlier court ruling {#us-title-ix-recodification-2026}

Event: 2026-09-29 (day precision). Action: regulation.

**What happened:** Following a September 28 announcement, the Department published its final recodification rule on September 29; that was also its effective date.

**Previously:** The 2024 rule had already been vacated, but its text remained in the Code of Federal Regulations.

**What changed:** The codified text was aligned with the earlier operative regime, with four specified exceptions. This was not the first day the Department stopped enforcing the 2024 rule.

**Scope:** 34 CFR part 106, governing Title IX obligations of recipients of federal education funding.

**Legal / procedural status:** Final recodification rule effective September 29, 2026.

**What applies now:** The prior text is recodified with exceptions for a previously vacated cross-examination sentence and obsolete provisions. 'Restored the 2020 rule' is shorthand, not a claim that every old sentence returned unchanged.

**Documented effect:** The published legal reference now reflects the restored text. The Department says this addresses confusion; no measured reduction in confusion or separate student-outcome effect was established here.

**Unresolved:** The earlier loss of the 2024 rule's protections may have consequences; this later housekeeping step does not establish their size or absence.

**Update when:** A new rule, operative court order, or documented effect of implementation changes the account.

First recorded: 2026-10-09. Last substantive revision: 2026-10-09. Last complete source check: 2026-10-09. Effective: 2026-09-29 (day precision). Jurisdiction: United States. Next review due: 2026-10-16.

## Arizona birth-certificate injunction stayed before its scheduled start {#us-az-birth-stay-2026}

Event: 2026-04-28 (day precision). Action: ruling.

**What happened:** The Ninth Circuit stayed the district court’s permanent injunction pending appeal.

**Previously:** The injunction would remove “operation” from the statute and regulation; its effective date had been delayed to April 30, 2026.

**What changed:** The broader administrative route did not take effect on that scheduled date.

**Scope:** Arizona-born people seeking amended birth certificates; the class challenged the surgery-verification route.

**Legal / procedural status:** Historical stay pending appeal; later merits opinion linked below.

**What applies now:** The October opinion rejected the facial challenge. Current mandate and any later stay remain unverified here.

**Documented effect:** The court preserved the prior procedural position instead of allowing the scheduled injunction to operate. These records do not measure applicants’ subsequent experiences.

**Unresolved:** An individual application’s outcome cannot be inferred from a stay. This is not a nationwide birth-certificate rule.

## FEC again loses its quorum, limiting enforcement and new litigation {#us-fec-quorum-loss-2025}

Event: 2025-04-30 (day precision). Action: institutional-capacity.

**What happened:** Allen Dickerson resigned on April 30, leaving three commissioners. A later departure on October 3 left two, both Democrats.

**Previously:** Four commissioners could consider matters requiring four affirmative votes, though votes could still deadlock.

**What changed:** The Commission could no longer authorize many enforcement, rulemaking, advisory-opinion and litigation actions. The later loss of bipartisan membership also restricted administrative decisions under Directive 10.

**Scope:** Federal campaign finance oversight; regulated committees, complainants, and the public seeking disclosure or agency decisions.

**Legal / procedural status:** Below quorum; current leadership page lists two commissioners at the source check.

**What applies now:** At the October 9, 2026 check, the FEC lists Shana Broussard and Dara Lindenbaum and four vacant seats. Staff services and disclosure work continue; the campaign finance laws were not repealed.

**Documented effect:** In Lacy v. FEC, the agency notified the court on July 22, 2025 that it lacked authority to authorize defense of that new action. This is a concrete operational effect, not a judgment accepting the plaintiffs' underlying allegations.

**Unresolved:** The inspector general documents impaired functions, but this entry does not quantify resulting violations, backlog growth, or electoral effects. An inability to authorize a new defense does not mean all previously authorized litigation stops.

**Update when:** A commissioner takes office, membership or voting rules change, or new official records document changed capacity or consequences.

## District court sets aside the entire 2024 Title IX rule {#us-title-ix-vacatur-2025}

Event: 2025-01-09 (day precision). Action: ruling.

**What happened:** In Tennessee v. Cardona, the Eastern District of Kentucky granted summary judgment to the challengers and rejected severing the disputed provisions from the rest of the rule.

**Previously:** Earlier injunctions prevented enforcement against covered plaintiffs and jurisdictions, rather than setting aside the rule itself everywhere.

**What changed:** The court's remedy operated on the entire rule nationwide. Its stated reasons concerned statutory authority, constitutional limits and arbitrary agency action; those are the court's holdings, not this timeline's endorsement of every argument.

**Scope:** The federal 2024 Title IX regulation for education programs receiving federal financial assistance; not every civil-rights protection or every institution's policy.

**Legal / procedural status:** Rule vacated in full; later agency recodification linked below.

**What applies now:** The 2024 rule remains set aside in the source record reviewed. The Department later formally restored the prior regulatory text; see the 2026 entry.

**Documented effect:** The Department's February 4, 2025 directive told recipients it would enforce the 2020 rule instead of the 2024 rule. That establishes the agency's enforcement instruction, not compliance by every school or a measurement of student outcomes.

**Unresolved:** Local policies, other legal protections and individual cases require separate examination. No population-wide outcome measurement was established in these sources. We retrieved the opinion from a public reproduction, not the complete live court docket.

**Update when:** A new operative court order, regulation, enforcement instruction, or documented institutional consequence changes this account.

First recorded: 2026-10-09. Last substantive revision: 2026-10-09. Last complete source check: No complete check; see access limits. Effective: 2025-01-09 (day precision). Jurisdiction: United States. Next review due: complete check needed.

Court opinion and amendment retrieved from public reproduction; current agency rule checked. Full appellate docket not independently inspected. Direct directive access failed, so no complete check is claimed.

## A commissioner takes office and briefly restores FEC capacity {#us-fec-quorum-restored-2020}

Event: 2020-06-05 (day precision). Action: institutional-capacity.

**What happened:** James E. Trainor III was sworn in as the fourth commissioner. The Senate had confirmed him on May 19; confirmation and taking office were different steps.

**Previously:** The Commission had lacked four sitting members since August 2019.

**What changed:** Decisions requiring four votes became possible again. Having enough members did not guarantee agreement.

**Scope:** Federal campaign finance regulation and FEC decision-making.

**Legal / procedural status:** Historical restoration; quorum lost again July 3, 2020.

**What applies now:** The restoration was temporary: Caroline Hunter's departure ended the quorum on July 3, 2020. This entry does not imply continuous capacity afterward.

**Documented effect:** For its brief FY2020 quorum, FEC management reported closing 33 enforcement matters, 39 alternative-dispute-resolution matters and 206 administrative-fine matters, with $842,413 in fines and penalties assessed.

**Unresolved:** The reported work is evidence of resumed action, not proof that the backlog vanished or enforcement became effective in every case.

**Update when:** A revised agency account or evidence requiring correction to the restoration's dates or outputs.

## FEC loses its voting quorum; filing obligations continue {#us-fec-quorum-loss-2019}

Event: 2019-08-31 (day precision). Action: institutional-capacity.

**What happened:** Matthew Petersen's departure left three sitting commissioners, below the four required for many Commission decisions.

**Previously:** The Commission had four members: the minimum voting quorum.

**What changed:** The agency lost decision-making capacity without Congress repealing campaign finance requirements.

**Scope:** Federal campaign finance regulation; committees, complainants, and people seeking FEC guidance. This does not describe the administration of every election.

**Legal / procedural status:** Historical loss of quorum; subsequent restoration and losses are linked below.

**What applies now:** This describes the 2019 episode, not an uninterrupted shutdown. See the linked restoration and 2025 loss.

**Documented effect:** The FEC's subsequent report says it could not decide enforcement cases, issue advisory opinions, or initiate rulemaking without four affirmative votes. Staff still processed reports and complaints and handled previously authorized work; reporting requirements remained in force.

**Unresolved:** These records establish impaired capacity, not the number of violations caused by the vacancy or whether any particular election result changed.

**Update when:** New archival evidence changing the dates or scope, or a correction to this account.
