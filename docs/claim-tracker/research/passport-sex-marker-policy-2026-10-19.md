## Claim and scope

**As circulating:** An October 19 Transitics article says the State Department investigates every passport applicant for a previous sex change. Its accompanying explanation also suggests the searches identify transgender people. [T1]

**Assessed assertion:** Do current U.S. passport instructions direct historical sex-marker searches, how broadly do those instructions apply, and what travel consequences are supported?

**Scope note:** Preserve the headline's universal scope when reviewing it. Evidence of a conditional search process cannot establish that every applicant receives additional database checks. A previous marker change also does not establish someone's gender identity. Passport adjudication, a dedicated transgender registry, criminal investigation, and monitoring someone's movements are distinct propositions.

**Draft assessment for a designated reviewer:** The blanket headline needs qualification. The birth-sex issuance rule and official warning of application delays are established. Published reproductions show conditional historical-record searches. A separate registry or broader surveillance purpose is unresolved, not established by these instructions. Complete the direct FAM source check before publishing a conclusive assessment of that manual's full current wording.

## What the evidence supports

State currently issues M/F passports reflecting sex assigned at birth. It warns that requests for X or a different marker can cause delays and further document requests. Existing passports remain valid until expiration, replacement, or invalidation under federal regulations; an updated or X marker does not automatically invalidate one. Some destinations separately restrict X-marker passports. [S1]

The indexed official instructions and reproduced excerpts list indicators including amended birth certificates, given-name changes or other given names customarily associated with another sex, blank or altered application sex fields, conflicting commercial records, and early renewal with more than two years remaining and sufficient visa pages. An indicator triggers mandatory ACRQ review and recommended further checks using LexisNexis, Nlets, EVVE, and/or Person-Centric Query Service; this does not require every listed database in every case. Indicators alone do not prove a prior marker change. [F1, F1a–F1b]

The October 24 indexed text adds material limits: extra research is unnecessary without indicators (-2(a)); discrepant requests require application review (-2(b)); once birth sex meets the preponderance standard, further sex-verification checks or suspension should stop (-2(h)). Missing or nonbinary birth-certificate markers alone are not conflicting information (-2(i)); amended evidence is not required (-2(j)). These are instructions, not measured compliance. The displayed version remains CT:CITZ-157, October 2, 2026; this does not date the first implementation of each instruction. Direct retrieval still failed. [F1]

The manual also requires annotating adjudicator changes in the application's issuance-office box (-2(d)). Recording an application decision is distinct from establishing a separate transgender registry. The inspected instructions do not establish secondary retention, sharing, or movement monitoring. [F1]

## Authority, challenge, and meaningful contrary evidence

The government's stated basis is Executive Order 14168 and its birth-sex identification rule, as described on State's current public guidance page. This identifies the administration's position; it does not settle every legal challenge. [S1]

On November 6, 2025, the Supreme Court stayed the June 17 district-court injunction. Its majority assessed the government as likely to succeed and characterized the marker as a historical fact; Justice Jackson, joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan, dissented. This was preliminary stay relief, not a final merits judgment. [C1, pages 1–2; C2, November 6 docket entries]

**Material correction to the October 19 legal-status account:** On June 5, 2026, the First Circuit dismissed appeal No. 25-1579 as moot and vacated the district court's preliminary-injunction orders after plaintiffs abandoned preliminary relief so trial proceedings could resume. This was not a merits decision upholding the policy. The November 2025 Supreme Court stay is relevant history, but an account ending there omits the later vacatur. The court-issued corrected judgment was read from counsel's hosted copy; the official court listing's June entry linked instead to its September 4, 2025 order. [C3]

A September 17, 2026 CM/ECF notice reproducing Judge Kobick's order grants Rule 56(d) discovery, sets fact discovery through March 31, 2027 and expert depositions through July 23, 2027, and states that pending summary-judgment motions will be denied without prejudice to renewal after discovery. It rejects the argument that the Supreme Court stay foreclosed the constitutional claims. Discovery relief neither restores the vacated injunctions nor finally resolves the policy's legality. This is a court-generated notice hosted by counsel, not direct access to the live trial docket. [C4]

The ACLU's October 2 case update remains an attributed account of continuing litigation. Its spring-2027 description is less precise than the September order's fact/expert schedule. No authenticated live PACER docket was inspected; the public CourtListener docket returned 403. A later modifying order therefore has not been ruled out. [A1, C4]

The existing-passport validity provision and the conditional nature of extra checks materially limit the broadest circulating interpretation. They do not eliminate the documented issuance restrictions or the risk of delay and unwanted disclosure.
