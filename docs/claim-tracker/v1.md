# Community claim tracker v1 specification

Draft revised after the initial summary, [full original review](review-detailed.md), and [follow-up review](FOLLOWUP.md). Prepared and revised October 9, 2026. See the historical [summary](review.md) and current [revision response](REVISIONS.md). The follow-up verdict was ready after specified revisions; this draft addresses the remaining written findings. ## Product purpose

Help someone who asks, "Is this true?" understand the claim as people encounter it, the evidence available, and what changes as the story develops. Community members submit claims and evidence. A designated human reviewer publishes a claim only with a first completed assessment, sources or an explicit explanation of insufficient evidence, uncertainty, and a visible revision history. Scheduled community discovery is a later gated stage.

The central promise is: **See what people are claiming, what the evidence supports so far, and what changed.** Popularity affects discovery and investigation priority. It does not determine an assessment.

## Pilot scope

The first release is manual-only: one niche, 10 to 20 invited participants, and one or two designated reviewers. Plan for up to five substantive reviews per working day across new claims, corrections, and evidence checks. These are proposed capacity limits, not demonstrated throughput or demand.

Use an invitation-only pilot. "Published" means visible to pilot participants. All queued, needs-context, and accepted-but-unassessed records remain visible only to their submitter and reviewers. Other participants can see a claim only after its first completed assessment is published. An explained unresolved outcome qualifies as a completed assessment; awaiting assessment does not. Internet publication is a later access-policy decision.

The first release includes text and link submissions, suggestions of published duplicates, manually proposed evidence, human assessments, corrections, following, an in-product update inbox, and review-due reminders. Automatic community imports, publisher-feed matching, and automatic evidence-change detection are a later stage. Screenshot uploads, anonymous submissions, community voting, email/push alerts, automated verdicts, paid subscriptions, and unrestricted crawling are deferred. Someone with a screenshot can submit a transcription and original link; describe the image's limitations.

Show no popularity or submission counts in the manual pilot. The initial niche and exact communities must be chosen before launch. Prefer factual claims about products, technology, or public policy with inspectable source material. Exclude identifying allegations about private individuals and individualized medical or legal advice from this pilot. Apply the same evidence criteria to every community and viewpoint.

## Participants and permissions

| Role | Permitted actions |
| --- | --- |
| Member | Read published records; submit claims and evidence; follow claims; request corrections; see their own submission status. |
| Reviewer | Triage submissions; define claims without changing their material meaning; assess evidence; publish and revise assessments; merge or archive records; resolve correction requests; request redaction and temporarily hide accidentally published sensitive material. Cannot erase published history. |
| Operator | Invite members; explicitly assign reviewer roles; monitor reviewer capacity, deadlines, and retention jobs; configure and disable connectors in the later stage. |
| Removal administrator | A separately granted permission for authorizing redaction or removal, identifying affected records, and disposing of emergency hides. May remove only prohibited text under a listed basis. Cannot erase revision labels, dates, stable reviewer handles, or the safe audit of that action. |
| Retention service | Purge only the private payloads due for deletion under the fixed retention policy; record completion. Cannot change assessments or purge published audit events. |
| Import service | Later-stage permission to read approved sources and admit bounded candidates or evidence suggestions. Disabled in the first release. |

Require authenticated identity for write actions. Authorization is enforced on the server for every operation. A tracker account is separate from an account on a monitored platform. Do not infer a participant's identity, affiliation, or beliefs from their activity in a source community. Import services cannot publish assessments or grant roles. The project owner explicitly grants removal-administrator permission; reviewer status alone never grants it. One pilot operator may hold multiple roles, but each privileged removal still requires that permission and a separate audit event. Label role overlap and whether the removal actor reviewed the affected assessment; it is never represented as a separate-person approval.

## Manual submission

The primary action is "Check a claim." Require the statement the person wants checked. Offer an optional source URL, community name, date, and brief explanation of why they are asking. A member-supplied community name is private reviewer context unless the submitter explicitly opts into its publication; the default is off. Collect only essential missing context, such as which city a policy concerns.

Preserve submitted wording privately where retention and source permissions allow. Before publication require three fields: **As circulating**, a reviewer-checked representation of the material assertion people encounter; **Assessed assertion**, the precise factual assertion evaluated; and **Scope note**, explaining any difference, including implied currency, place, quantity, and qualifications. Redact identifying or restricted details without changing material meaning. If meaning cannot be represented safely, decline publication. A post with three factual assertions may produce three linked private candidates. Opinions and predictions can be declined as outside scope; a checkable factual premise within them can be extracted.

Members receive possible matches only from already published records they are authorized to read. Private candidates, accepted-but-unassessed claims, declined records, other members' identifiers, and private match counts never appear in suggestions or member-visible search. A member can propose attaching an observation to a published claim. Reviewers may compare private candidates within their role and confirm an association. Similarity alone never authorizes a merge. Unknown and inaccessible IDs produce the same member-facing response; duplicate APIs and counts must not reveal the existence of a private record.

Receipt copy should say: "Submitted for review. Other participants can see a claim after its first assessment." The submitter sees private progress; following by other members begins only for a published claim. A declined submission includes a safe reason and remains private until its payload is purged under the retention policy.

## Review capacity and priority

Allow at most two open new submissions per member and 20 open unassessed claims across the pilot. A multi-claim submission consumes one slot per resulting claim; request splitting if it would exceed the limit. When the queue is full, decline admission without retaining the attempted payload, explain the backlog, and keep the published-claim correction route available. These limits are initial settings that may be lowered to match observed capacity. The age pause and correction priority govern admission first; 20 is a hard safety ceiling, not a backlog target or throughput promise.

Reviewer-confirmed specific correction reports, reviewer-detected retractions, and privacy/removal tasks take priority over new intake. Untriaged member reports remain private; quick specificity triage may use a reserved priority slot, but only a specific report enters substantive reassessment or creates a public banner. Reserve the first two daily review slots for that lane; use more or all slots when urgent corrections require them. If no such work is pending, unused slots may serve new claims. Popularity never displaces an outstanding correction. Treat overdue evidence checks ahead of new claims. Pause new intake when the oldest unassessed record exceeds three working days; disclose the pause without revealing private contents. Retain admitted correction tasks until explicit disposition rather than expiring them with new candidates. Allow at most two open correction reports per member, counted from submission through triage and resolution separately from new-submission limits. Refuse a third without retaining its attempted payload and explain the limit; members may add relevant detail to an existing report. Coalesce reports of the same evidence change into one review task. A dismissed report gives its reporter a safe reason. Urgent confidential privacy/removal escalation to the operator stays available without an automatic banner or power to hide history; reviewer-initiated tasks are not blocked by a member cap.

Untriaged manual candidates and needs-context records expire after 14 calendar days without progress; a reviewer-requested extension must record a reason and a new deadline. Expiry is a private disposition, not an assessment. Accepted-but-unpublished records have a named reviewer and a first-assessment deadline no later than three working days after acceptance. If no first assessment is published by that deadline plus three calendar days, transition to Expired and schedule payload deletion. Editing, reassignment, or reopening cannot reset that clock; no extension applies to this accepted-state grace period. Published dated assessments do not expire by this rule. Archiving or merging an unpublished record also closes its private payload for deletion within seven calendar days. Payload retention is defined below.

## Later stage automatic discovery

No connector, polling, or publisher-feed matching is required for the manual-only first release. This later stage can start only after manual release checks pass, reviewer capacity is demonstrated, and the source permissions and queue controls below are verified. The source and scheduling numbers below are proposed limits for the later stage.

A community source identifies a particular subreddit, forum category, account/list, or community feed. Store its stable identifier, display name, permitted access method, coverage, and access state. A platform name alone does not identify a community.

Start with one authorized community connector. Expansion to three to five sources requires measured spare capacity. Default to polling every 30 minutes, adjusted to provider limits. Scan at most 50 new items per source per run; scanning does not admit every post to the human queue. Admit at most three new candidates per source per day, with at most three pending per source and ten pending automated candidates globally. The pilot's overall 20-claim limit still applies. Once any limit is reached, stop admission, record the period and counts omitted where known, and display a coverage gap. Do not retain an unbounded staging queue outside those limits.

Persist an admitted item before advancing its cursor; retries must not create duplicate observations. Record explicitly skipped items or skipped ranges before advancing over them, so coverage limitations are observable. When scanning is paused, preserve the cursor; if the provider later cannot return older items, record that gap. Record last attempt, last success, failure reason, and next attempt. Respect provider backoff and prevent overlapping runs for one source. Use a run lease with a fencing token; reject a stale run’s writes and cursor updates even if its process continues after lease expiry. Resume only when capacity is available and urgent correction work is clear.

Imports create private source-post candidates within the admission limits. Expire untriaged automated candidates after seven days; record the missed review and purge their payloads on the retention schedule. The reviewer selects the atomic claim and confirms the as-circulating representation and context. Automated extraction may suggest wording but never publishes claims or assessments. Import a source item once using a stable source-item identity; multiple claims may refer to that item, but every resulting candidate must fit the queue caps. Scheduling and importing cannot reserve or consume correction capacity. Source-item edits produce a dated observed version under the same item identity, within existing storage and queue caps; they cannot silently rewrite the prior observation or assessment. Required source deletions use the platform-required-deletion removal basis and purge prohibited wording from current and historical copies.

After verifying the first connector, add at most five permitted publisher feeds for developing coverage. Match incoming articles to published claims as evidence suggestions, subject to reviewer confirmation. Admit at most two pending automatic evidence suggestions per claim and ten globally; excess items are counted as omitted where possible, without an unlimited hidden backlog. Record coverage gaps. New coverage never automatically changes an assessment. Manually proposed evidence and member correction reports remain available in the first release and take precedence when they concern a potentially invalid published assessment.

Only label a source "enabled" after permission, credentials where required, rate limits, and an end-to-end import have been verified. A fixture import is labeled test data. Page refresh, manual file import, and a saved schedule alone do not demonstrate unattended operation.

Reddit requires explicit approval for API access; commercial use requires written approval and the applicable agreement. Keep its adapter disabled until the intended use is authorized. [Responsible Builder Policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/42728983564564-Responsible-Builder-Policy), [Data API Terms](https://redditinc.com/policies/data-api-terms).

Truth Social's terms restrict systematic collection without written permission and prohibit specified automated extraction. Keep its adapter disabled until an authorized access arrangement covers the proposed use. [Truth Social Terms of Service](https://help.truthsocial.com/legal/terms-of-service/).

These source requirements were checked October 9, 2026. Recheck terms and permissions before enabling each connector. Manual submissions do not authorize the tracker to copy or retrieve restricted platform content.

## Intake state and assessment state

Track workflow separately from evidence assessment.

| Intake state | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Queued | Received and awaiting triage. |
| Needs context | Essential claim details are missing; remain private. |
| Accepted | Specific and relevant; a reviewer has accepted it for assessment. It remains private until a completed first assessment is published. |
| Declined | Outside scope or unsuitable, with a recorded reason. |
| Merged | Associated with an equivalent existing claim, with a destination and merge event. |
| Archived | Retained where permitted; no routine monitoring, with a reason and date. |
| Expired | Private new intake closed after its recorded deadline; no evidentiary conclusion, with payload deletion due. |

Queued and needs-context items may be accepted, declined, merged, or expired. Accepted claims may be merged or archived; accepted-but-unpublished records also expire under the first-assessment deadline and grace rule. Every terminal unpublished record enters the seven-day payload-deletion schedule, including archived or merged records. Track visibility separately as private, published, or temporarily hidden. Only a reviewer publishing a first completed assessment can transition a private claim to published. Reopening creates an explicit review event without automatically restoring publication or purged content. A multi-claim submission tracks each resulting candidate separately.

| Assessment | Required explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Awaiting assessment | Internal only; no completed assessment has been published and the claim is inaccessible to other participants. |
| Supported | Reviewed evidence supports this exact scoped assertion as of the assessment date; state limits and material contrary evidence. |
| Contradicted | Reviewed evidence directly conflicts with the assertion; explain the conflict. |
| Misleading | Identify the accurate component and the omission, framing, or qualification that changes the meaning. |
| Unresolved | A reviewer examined the question but available evidence is insufficient or conflicting; state what remains unknown and what would help resolve it. |

Supported, contradicted, and misleading require reviewed evidence with precise references and a rationale. Unresolved can be published when no sufficient evidence exists, with the review performed and limitation explained. Missing evidence never defaults to contradicted. Do not display numeric truth or confidence scores.

The record's headline assessment answers the **as-circulating** assertion with its material context intact. A narrower supported fact cannot substitute for a broader or more current assertion. For example, "the city just banned gardens" cannot receive Supported solely because a 2019 ordinance restricts planting on city-owned sidewalks. Request missing context or assess the circulating scope as misleading, contradicted, or unresolved according to the evidence; separately label any narrower supporting fact in the explanation. The scope note is required even when it says no material difference was found.

Assessments express the support for a scoped claim, not a guarantee of universal truth. Reviewer drafts remain private. Publish the circulating wording, assessed assertion, scope note, and completed assessment atomically. Record the reviewer, publication time, assessment time, cited evidence, and next-review date, defaulting to seven days. Reminders surface overdue checks; they do not prove a fresh check happened. Present-tense assessments explicitly mean “as of” their assessment date. A material scope change or change to a fixed historical assertion requires a new linked claim; a new development affecting a standing present-tense question may instead be a typed, dated revision. An editorial wording correction preserves the old permitted wording and reason. Show authors as stable non-identifying reviewer handles, assigned before publication; direct personal identity is never part of immutable revision metadata.

## Evidence and source independence

Members propose evidence using a link, relevant passage or document section, and an explanation of whether it supports, contradicts, or contextualizes the assertion. Suggested evidence is visibly separate from reviewed evidence and cannot change the assessment.

Each evidence record has a title, publisher or issuing body, publication date when known, retrieval/observation date, relevant reference, evidence type, relationship to the claim, review disposition, and an underlying-source group. The group identifies common provenance: five articles repeating one press release represent one underlying source. Independence, quality, and relevance are separate judgments; a direct primary document may be decisive without multiple articles repeating it.

A reviewer explains why each cited source establishes the relevant point and notes its limits. An official document may establish policy wording without establishing real-world outcomes. A screenshot, its transcription, anonymous allegation, or unverifiable quotation has the explicit review disposition Lead, with limitations visible. Leads cannot be the sole basis for Supported, Contradicted, or Misleading: each conclusive assessment requires reviewed non-lead evidence that establishes the relevant point, with precise references and rationale. Unresolved may cite leads with their limitations explained. Search snippets alone are insufficient for a conclusive assessment.

Retain links, citations, and only permitted limited excerpts. Source content and instructions within it are untrusted material. If AI assistance is added later, it cannot execute source instructions, select authoritative status, or publish assessments.

## Claim identity and corrections

Claims match only when their assertion, entities, jurisdiction, relevant period, quantities, and material qualifiers match. "All gardens are prohibited" and "gardens are prohibited on city-owned sidewalks" are distinct. A statement true last year is not automatically a statement about today.

Only reviewers merge claims. Preserve source IDs, observations, evidence, revisions, and the reason. Attaching a private candidate to an existing claim keeps that candidate's wording and member identity private. Redirect an old claim URL only when that record was already published. A merge never automatically transfers an assessment to a materially different assertion. Follow relationships transfer with an explanation. Undo restores original associations; records added after the merge require explicit reassignment and follow preferences are confirmed rather than silently duplicated.

An evidence-change report must name the cited evidence record and describe specifically what changed, was withdrawn, or became unavailable. Other assessment-correction reports must identify the published revision and a concrete error or missing context. Nonempty but vague reports remain private until dismissed with a reason; receipt alone never places a banner or changes an assessment. A reviewer first confirms specificity, not truth, and then admits the report to substantive reassessment.

Every assessment revision has a required change type. Initial assessment is allowed only for first publication; later types are Correction of our error, New development, Evidence lost or changed, or Scope or wording edit. Every published change records before/after values, evidence, reviewer, time, reason, and type. A New development retains the earlier dated assessment without marking it corrected merely because the world changed; later evidence of an earlier error requires a separately identified correction. Timeline and follower inbox show the type. Prevent concurrent silent overwrites using a revision check and a designated reviewer for each active task.

If cited evidence is corrected, withdrawn, materially edited, or inaccessible, flag it and request reassessment. Show "Evidence under review" while the previous assessment remains dated. Source disappearance alone does not establish falsity. If a reviewer confirms that sole decisive support was retracted or otherwise invalidated, immediately publish an unresolved revision explaining the lost support while reassessment continues. If remaining reviewed evidence still supports the claim, explain that basis. Withdrawing a published assessment requires a visible revision and reason; it cannot silently erase the prior assessment.

In the first release, changes are detected through specific member reports and reviewers manually checking cited pages at review-due dates. Closing a scheduled review requires a dated unchanged/changed/unavailable result for every cited evidence record; an overdue reminder is not detection. Only after a reviewer confirms an evidence-change report is specific does the claim display "Evidence change reported; review pending"; the notice explicitly means specificity was checked, not that the source changed. Reviewer confirmation of an actual change uses "Evidence under review" and the confirmed check date. Dismissal removes the pending-report banner when no other admitted report requires it. Reviewers manually confirm availability, wording changes, or retractions. There is no promise of automatic detection or comprehensive monitoring. Later approved connectors may propose detected changes within their source scope; arbitrary server fetching of submitted evidence links remains prohibited.

## Removal authority and retention

Reviewers can request redaction and temporarily hide an accidentally published sensitive record. The emergency hide requires a suspected listed removal basis and a safe audit event, replaces sensitive content with a neutral availability notice while retaining each revision’s safe label, dates, and reviewer handle, and must be sent to a removal administrator for disposition within 24 hours. If disposition is overdue, the material stays hidden and the operator is alerted; it is never automatically republished. Reviewer permission cannot erase earlier assessments or delete the hide event.

Only a removal administrator authorizes redaction or removal of published payloads. The closed bases are Private-person data, Legal request, Platform-required deletion, and Copyright; reject any other basis. Record a safe rationale, actor, time, basis, affected IDs, and effect on the assessment in an audit entry that cannot be erased by the acting reviewer or administrator. Only the specific personal or restricted text may be removed, replaced in each affected revision by "[removed: basis]". Each revision’s assessment label, assessment and publication dates, and stable non-identifying reviewer handle are unremovable and remain visible even while sensitive content is hidden. Dissatisfaction with an earlier assessment is never a removal basis. A removal event states if its administrator also holds a reviewer role and explicitly labels "Removal by the reviewing author" when the same person reviewed the affected assessment. Redaction covers current pages, assessment history, excerpts, follower events, search results, and operational copies and any operator-created export. If material evidence must be removed, publish a safe explanation of the resulting uncertainty or hide the record when it cannot be represented safely. Avoid naming a private person in a removal reason.

Purge identifying private-person allegation payloads as soon as practical and no later than seven calendar days after decline. Apply the same seven-day maximum after terminal disposition to all never-published private submission and draft-claim payloads, including declined, expired, archived, or merged records. Accepted-but-never-published records expire after their first-assessment deadline plus grace period and are purged within seven calendar days of that expiry. This covers original wording, identity details, source URLs that identify the allegation, excerpts, revisions, attachments if later supported, generated summaries, and logs. Logs must never capture submission bodies by default. Retain only a content-free disposition event with an opaque ID, safe reason category, action dates, actor, and deletion completion; do not keep hashes of sensitive text as audit substitutes.

The retention service runs daily and alerts the operator on missed deadlines. The hosting/storage choice must provide deletion from active storage and operational copies within the seven-day limit. Backups have a maximum 30-day expiry, restricted access, and a deletion manifest applied before any restored data becomes accessible. A provider that cannot honor this policy blocks the manual pilot's launch. A required legal preservation exception must be separately documented and access-restricted; it does not authorize public exposure. Published material has a separate, audited removal path above. Correction tasks and safe audit events do not expire merely because their payload is removed.

## Screens and follower updates

1. **Tracker:** Search published assessed claims only; filter by assessment and community; show as-circulating wording, scope note, assessment date, last material update, and review-due indicator. Describe the manual-only coverage.
2. **Check a claim:** Submit wording and context; inspect authorized published matches; receive private status and any capacity warning.
3. **Claim detail:** As circulating, assessed assertion, and scope note alongside the assessment and explanation; supporting and contrary reviewed evidence; suggested evidence separately; dated timeline; follow and request-correction actions.
4. **Following:** An in-product inbox for published assessment changes, material reviewed-evidence updates, and merge/archive notices. Display what changed, why, the required change type, and the revision link. A label change, material reviewed-evidence change, merge, or archive always notifies followers. Every other revision requires an explicit notify-followers decision and reason. Create exactly one durable inbox event per active follower at the revision’s commit, despite retries; committed unfollow prevents future events. No alerts for every unreviewed submission.
5. **Reviewer queue:** Priority corrections, retractions, privacy reports, and overdue evidence checks before new private candidates; show capacity and expiry dates.
6. **Sources:** In the later stage, show coverage gaps, admission caps, access state, polling interval, last successful import, errors, and disabled sources. The first release explicitly shows automatic discovery as unavailable.

Label attribution "First observed by this tracker in..." rather than claiming to have found the original source. In the manual release, publish member-supplied community context only with the submitter’s explicit opt-in, identify it as member-supplied, and state its verification limits. Reviewer-recorded public sightings may be attributed with a date and link; neither implies a live feed. Private context never enters filters, counts, follower events, or exports. Later imported item counts state their period, community, and deduplication method. Avoid "most popular" or comparative growth until coverage and historical baselines justify those labels. Cross-platform engagement units remain separate. Corrections and overdue evidence checks have the priority defined above; repeated submissions cannot establish credibility.

| --- | --- |
| Source | Community/publisher identity, method, permission state, scope, interval, cursor, operational status. |
| Submission or observation | Private original permitted wording, member/service origin, source-item key where applicable, source link, context, observed time, candidate associations, expiry and payload-deletion dates. |
| Claim | Stable ID, as-circulating representation, assessed assertion, scope note and qualifiers, intake state, visibility, current assessment revision, owner, first-assessment deadline/grace/expiry, review due date, deletion due date if unpublished and closed. |
| Evidence | Source metadata, precise citation, underlying-source group, relation to claim, disposition, availability. |
| Assessment revision | Claim, assessment label, scoped explanation, evidence references, stable non-identifying author handle, assessment/publication dates, previous revision, required change type and reason, notify decision, removal markers. | Review event | Triage, merge/undo, specific correction report, resolution, emergency hide, removal authorization, or retention completion; actor, safe reason/listed basis, affected IDs, specificity decision, role-overlap/same-reviewer flag, deadline, and deletion completion without prohibited payloads. |
| Follow and follower event | Member/claim association, material revision reference, unread state. |
| Import run | Later-stage source, start/end, cursor, scanned/admitted/omitted counts, coverage gaps, cap/expiry status, outcome, retry state, lease/fencing token, source-item version/deletion handling. |
| Membership | Account identity and explicitly assigned role. |

Use shared durable server storage for community records. Browser storage alone cannot meet the shared tracker requirement. Keep reviewer operations atomic with their audit events and reject stale writes. The stack, hosting provider, and AI provider remain implementation decisions; this specification does not require an LLM.

Render user content as text and accept only validated HTTP/HTTPS links. Source connectors use configured approved hosts; do not fetch arbitrary submitted URLs on the server. Restrict redirects, request size, time, and private-network access for any future fetcher. Show outbound destination hosts and use `rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow ugc"` on user-supplied links. Apply submission limits and a private abuse-review path. Member-facing export/download is outside the pilot; any operator-created operational export is tracked, access-restricted, and subject to the same redaction and retention rules. Explain blocked submissions without exposing other members' private records.

## Acceptance criteria

M checks gate the manual-only first release. L checks gate the later automatic stage. Preserve A identifiers for continuity with the initial review.

| A2 | L | One authorized connector runs while the browser is closed; admitted candidates retain source identity and observation time; last-success status matches the run. It remains disabled during M. |
| A3 | L | Partial failure and concurrent retries do not duplicate or skip admitted observations. Expired-lease writes/cursor updates fail; source edits retain dated versions without duplicate items; required source deletions remove prohibited wording. Saturation respects caps, pauses, skipped coverage, expiry, and correction capacity. |

| A5 | M | Members can suggest contrary evidence but cannot review it, publish assessments, change roles, or erase history. Inaccessible/nonexistent IDs give the same response. Reviewer-only credentials cannot invoke removal administration; an unlisted removal basis fails. All M checks must pass before the manual pilot launches. Demonstrate A2, A3, and the L portion of A12 with one authorized live source before describing automatic discovery as available. Written requirements and saved schedules do not constitute passed checks.

## Delivery order and pilot evaluation

Build identity, role checks, durable records, bounded private manual intake, safe matching, and retention first. Add first-assessment publication with as-circulating scope, evidence review, corrections, removal administration, following, and review reminders. Release the manual pilot after M checks pass. Only then consider one authorized scheduled connector after verifying L queue controls and observed spare capacity; expand sources gradually.

The private pilot tests reviewer workflow and invited-member understanding; it cannot establish reach or impact in the wider source communities. For a four-week manual pilot, measure time to first human review, private backlog age, capacity pauses/expiry, traceable evidence coverage, correction resolution time, retention completion, and whether followers return to read developments. Ask whether the circulating assertion, scope differences, and remaining uncertainty are clear and whether updates changed understanding. Do not optimize the fraction labeled supported or contradicted.
