## Corrections before publication

### 1. Tennessee: "two-year" should be "at least two years," and the knowledge point can be firmer

**Passage:** "The State said he already knew Lee's sex assigned at birth during their two-year sexual relationship; this was not a sudden-discovery account."

The opinion records the State's motion as averring a sexual relationship "for at least two years" (p. 1). That is an averment, not a court finding. Ward's own side also confirms the knowledge. At the hearing on whether he would testify, the State stipulated that his testimony would show "this added level of fear because he knew of ... the assigned sex at birth of the victim" (p. 5). The court also relies on it: the jury "was without knowledge of a significant fact known to Defendant" (p. 11).

**Correction:** "According to the State's motion, the two had a sexual relationship for at least two years, and both sides accepted that Ward knew Lee's sex assigned at birth. This was not a sudden-discovery account."

### 2. Tennessee: say the trial judge ruled the evidence irrelevant

**Passage:** "The trial judge considered a gun dangerous regardless of the holder's sex and sought to avoid distracting or prejudicing the jury."

The judge said, "I just don't believe that gender is relevant here," and "I do not want a mistrial. I do not want confusion of the issues" (p. 3). The relevance ruling is what the appellate court reversed first (pp. 8–9), so readers need it to follow the next sentence. "Distracting or prejudicing" also drifts from the judge's own words.

**Correction:** "The trial judge ruled that sex was not relevant here, saying a deadly weapon is deadly whoever holds it, and wanted to avoid confusing the issues or causing a mistrial."

### 3. Tennessee: include the court's "misled the jury" reasoning

The article gives one of the court's two prejudice arguments: bias could cut against Ward because of the relationship. It leaves out the other, which the court treats as central. Excluding the evidence "left the jury with the impression that a male defendant killed a female victim" and so "misled the jury" (p. 10). The court then held the error was not harmless (p. 12).

Leaving this out makes the court's reasoning look thinner than it is. That would undercut the article's own fairness standard ("does not require ... assuming discriminatory intentions by the judges").

**Correction:** After "It also argued that possible bias could affect him because of their sexual relationship," add: "It concluded that withholding the information misled the jury by leaving the impression that a male defendant had killed a female victim, and that the error was not harmless."

### 4. Tennessee: the court's no-detail statement should be its own words

**Passage:** "The opinion also invoked general physiological differences between sexes without detailing scientific support."

This is accurate. The court went further, though, and said detail was unnecessary: "A detailed discussion is not necessary for our analysis; it suffices to acknowledge that these differences exist" (p. 8). Quoting it is fairer to the court, and it is sharper evidence for the article's point.

**Correction:** "The opinion invoked general physiological differences between the sexes, saying 'a detailed discussion is not necessary for our analysis.'"

### 5. Tennessee: add a currency line and recheck the docket

The decision is four days old. The State can still seek permission to appeal to the Tennessee Supreme Court; under TRAP 11, I understand the window to be 60 days from the judgment. The editorial note already says to recheck the docket. The article itself should also carry a date stamp so the text stays true if review is sought.

**Correction:** Open the section with "As of [publication date], ..." or close it with "Further appeal is possible." Recheck tncourts.gov before publishing.

### 6. Part Three: all six cases are American, not "mostly"

**Passage:** "These six cases are mostly American legal and institutional examples."

All six are U.S. cases, and so is the Tennessee case. **Correction:** "These six cases are all American legal and institutional examples."

### 7. Bob Jones: rewrite the awkward lesson sentence and match the Court's wording

**Passage:** "A claim of infringed religious freedom can concern withdrawal of a tax benefit because of discriminatory institutional conduct."

This sentence is hard to parse, and it repeats the one before it. Separately, "It recognized a burden on religious exercise" slightly overstates the Court. The Court acknowledged that denying the benefits would have "a substantial impact" on religious schools. It held that the government's interest "substantially outweighs whatever burden" denial imposes.

**Correction:** Delete the awkward sentence. Change the earlier one to "It acknowledged that losing tax benefits would substantially affect religious schools, but held that the government's interest in ending racial discrimination in education outweighed whatever burden that imposed."
