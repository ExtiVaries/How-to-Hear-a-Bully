# How we check our work

*Methods page prepared 9 October 2026. This date describes this page, not a new review of every linked resource.*

[Practical Guides](homepage.md) brings together free resources for understanding a situation, checking your options and choosing a next step. The shared aim is to make evidence and limits easy to inspect. No label can guarantee that a resource is unbiased or error-free; readers should be able to question how we reached a conclusion.

## What each project covers

- **[Don't Pay a Middleman](https://dont-pay-a-middleman.vercel.app/)** is an independent directory of official U.S. federal service routes. Entries distinguish government fees from payments to a helper and explain what the linked service can establish. Paid advice, representation, translation or other help may be valuable. The directory does not decide your eligibility or replace advice about your circumstances.
- **[How to Hear a Bully](../../how-to-hear-a-bully.md)** examines manipulative uses of language, with historical context, political cases and practical exercises. It distinguishes harmful patterns from disagreement, mistakes and honest clarification. The same scrutiny should apply to speakers you support and speakers you oppose.
- **[Why Does Everything Feel Like a Crisis?](../crisis-guide/why-does-everything-feel-like-a-crisis.md)** helps readers separate events, information, unequal exposure to harm and possible responses. Its questions can support greater concern as well as reassurance. They are teaching material, not a clinical treatment or a validated danger score. Current emergency instructions take priority.
- **[Who to Trust When It All Breaks Down?](../trust-guide/who-to-trust-when-it-all-breaks-down.md)** helps readers choose whom to rely on for a particular question when accounts conflict. It examines expertise, evidence, repeated reports, interests, corrections and uncertainty. Its five questions are an untested teaching aid, not a validated trust score. Immediate danger requires protective action and emergency help without delay.

These resources help you examine a claim or find a route. They do not certify businesses, diagnose people or establish anyone's motives merely from a disagreement.

The collection also links to [Plant Climate Map](https://plantclimatemap.org/) under **Climate and growing**. It is a separate app for exploring climate classifications and plant hardiness zones. Its map information and data sources remain on its own site; the guide review dates and reuse terms below do not cover the map or its data.

## Sources, interpretation and uncertainty

Primary sources are the starting point where available: an agency's requirements, an original statement, a historical text in an identified translation, or a research paper. Context and relevant contrary evidence matter too. A primary source is evidence of what it actually records; it does not make every claim inside it true.

The [Bully notes](../../how-to-hear-a-bully-notes.md) include sources, case records, unresolved questions and a revision ledger. The [Crisis notes](../crisis-guide/why-does-everything-feel-like-a-crisis-notes.md) explain what each source supports and the limits of that support. The [seven Trust notes](../trust-guide/who-to-trust-when-it-all-breaks-down-notes.md) distinguish research findings from editorial advice and record unresolved source questions, including the 2022 classroom study's allocation method. Middleman entries identify sources, check dates and fields that remain unverified; its [own methods page](https://dont-pay-a-middleman.vercel.app/about#verification) gives the details.

Facts, interpretations and practical suggestions have different roles. A quotation establishes wording, not necessarily intent. An observational association does not by itself establish cause. A study of clicks does not measure anxiety, and an official definition does not validate an exercise built around it. The Crisis and Trust guides' invented scenarios are labeled as such.

Reading a paper is not the same as reproducing its analysis. Where access was incomplete or a finding remains uncertain, the notes say so. Our proposed questions and explanations should be judged alongside their evidence, including evidence that might change the conclusion.

## Check spending authority before accepting a savings claim {#spending-authority}

*A source-checking guide using dated historical records, not a current-outcome tracker or a judgment about whether a program deserves funding. Last checked October 29, 2026. The title describes a general accountability problem; it is not a quotation attributed to Vought or another official.*

**Why these examples:** They were found through [Who He Hired's Vought profile](https://whohehired.com/people/vought) and [Rubio profile](https://whohehired.com/people/rubio), a directory of one administration's hires and selected for checkable official decisions, competing legal arguments, and fiscal records. The inclusion test is whether readers can reproduce the comparison and its limits, regardless of party. This selection cannot establish relative wrongdoing across administrations.

When an administration proposes a spending cut, two different questions arise: Is the change desirable, and who has authority to make it? A reader can favor a policy change while still asking whether the required process was followed. Conversely, a procedural defect does not prove that every affected program was well designed. Keep those questions separate so a debate about efficiency does not quietly substitute for a check on legal authority.

Start with the appropriation and the proposed action. Identify the account, amount, fiscal year, availability period, and the official document changing or delaying obligations. Then distinguish a proposal to rescind funds from an enacted rescission. Under [2 U.S.C. §683](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t2/s683/), a presidential special message supplies specified information to Congress; amounts proposed for rescission must be made available unless Congress completes action on a rescission bill rescinding the relevant amount within the prescribed statutory period. The statute's timing rules need careful application, not a simple countdown assumed from a headline.

[Who He Hired's Vought profile](https://whohehired.com/people/vought) provides two GAO leads, but the original decisions are more precise than a person-centered summary. On September 29, 2026, [GAO concluded](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-338788) that the September 25 rescission proposals did not authorize withholding the affected appropriations beyond FY2026. This was a pre-expiration legal conclusion, not proof of later withholding. GAO took no position on the programs' merits. It remains a dated legal opinion, not a court judgment or a current account-by-account outcome report.

The executive branch contests that interpretation. Its [September 2025 Supreme Court application](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25A269/373945/20250908080903468_GHC_Application_9.8_Final.pdf) argued that the ICA permits withholding throughout the statutory consideration period even when appropriations expire first. The [White House again claimed ICA authority](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/president-trump-takes-historic-action-to-eliminate-wasteful-and-harmful-spending/) for the September 2026 action.

The Supreme Court's [September 26, 2025 order](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/25a269_bp7c.pdf) in *Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition* stayed an injunction only as to funding covered by the August 28 special message. It found a sufficient early showing that the ICA precluded those respondents' APA suit and that mandamus was unavailable. The Court expressly withheld a final merits determination. This was not a holding that all late rescissions are lawful, nor a ruling on the September 2026 proposal.

Later events require their own evidence. A [seven-state complaint](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/1-complaint.pdf) filed September 30, 2026 alleges approximately $426 million in the refugee account was already obligated, using an August snapshot. That is a pleading, not a finding or today's account balance. Expiration of authority for new obligations should not be confused with disappearance of existing obligated balances.

HHS's [October 22 workbook](https://portal.max.gov/portal/document/SF133/Budget/attachments/2692285459/FY2026_SF133_MONTHLY_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services.xlsx) reports account 75-1503 24/26's unobligated balance rising from $141.1 million in August to $567.5 million in September; cumulative reported recoveries of prior-year unpaid obligations rise from $194.0 million to $562.9 million. The September balance is $51,024.70 above the [proposed $567,401,904 rescission](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/September-Rescissions-Package-for-Speaker-Johnson_signed-9.25.26.pdf). Its $426.34 million rise is numerically close to the complaint's roughly $426 million already-obligated figure; reported recoveries rose $368.85 million. These are accounting comparisons, not payments to beneficiaries. Numerical proximity does not identify transactions or establish causes, October activity, legal effects, or effects on state awards. The suit's disposition and treatment of all 11 accounts remain unverified. In the workbook's “TAFS detail” sheet, August and September columns T–U, account 75-1503 24/26, line 2490 row 37122 shows $141,114,957.35 and $567,452,928.70; line 1021 row 37108 shows cumulative recoveries of $194,032,466.50 and $562,882,848.10. The linked [FY2026 SF 133 reports page](https://portal.max.gov/portal/document/SF133/Budget/FY%202026%20-%20SF%20133%20Reports%20on%20Budget%20Execution%20and%20Budgetary%20Resources.html) identifies the reporting source.

In a separate [August 5, 2025 decision](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-337203), GAO concluded that HHS/NIH withholding violated the Impoundment Control Act. The decision did not determine whether NIH had authority to terminate particular grants. These are findings about agency conduct and appropriations procedure, not criminal findings or personal-liability judgments against Vought.

The [current CRS account](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13131) is [version 5, dated April 17, 2026](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13131/IF13131.5.pdf), not the December 2025 version used in the first packet. CRS describes NIH as having ultimately “spent” its FY2025 budget authority; the cited figure measures grant and Other Transaction award values. That evidence concerns award obligations, not cash payments to recipients or SF 133 accounting recoveries. CRS also reports continuing disruption: 1,392 terminated awards with $539 million in unliquidated obligations as of April 10. It notes reinstatement of many previously terminated or paused grants, without establishing that all or most of those 1,392 awards were restored. More upfront multiyear funding affects comparisons. Its slower-FY2026 observation is an April snapshot, not the year's final outcome. None of this erases GAO's earlier finding or proves which individual grants were restored. The direct PDF fetch was unavailable in the source review; official HTML and version history were checked. Changed wording does not itself prove a retraction.

Use a small worksheet rather than a sweeping verdict. Write down what the official announced, what action the records show, the claimed legal authority, and the strongest contrary evidence. Record whether a source is an agency's own explanation, GAO's legal conclusion, a litigant's allegation, or a court order. If the records do not establish a link to a named person's decision, leave that link unresolved rather than filling it with a job title.

Make the worksheet reproducible: save the direct link, account, page, version, and check date. Check version history, not only a fresh search crawl. A later omission of wording does not itself prove a retraction. Do not compare totals covering different periods or categories without reconciliation.

**A calm question:** “Which enacted provision permits this withholding or cancellation, what is the deadline, and where can we see the account-level action and any congressional response?”

Do not stop at an announcement or a favorable opinion. Distinguish availability, obligations, payments, and services delivered; distinguish legal merits from who can sue for which remedy. A later release may answer one question while leaving delay or effects unresolved. This method is not a trust score or a shortcut to intent. Use it when the administration or your policy preference changes.

## How historical examples are selected {#example-selection}

Choose teachable mechanisms with an original record, adequate context, a strong alternative reading, and explicit outcome limits, regardless of party or administration. The [Rubio rhetoric example](/How-to-Hear-a-Bully/rhetoric-examples.html#rubio-visa-label-2025-03-27) and the spending example above were discovered through one administration's directory. This small pilot is illustrative, not representative or comparative; it cannot support prevalence estimates or rankings. Broaden future discovery through [GAO's topic-based Impoundment Control Act index](https://www.gao.gov/legal/appropriations-law/impoundment-control-act), which includes decisions across administrations, including no-violation findings. The index is a discovery channel, not a complete or unbiased sample. A comparable example from another administration needs separate research; do not invent one or force equal counts.

For each candidate, log its **discovery source**, mechanism, administration, primary-source basis, and reason for including or excluding it. Record the source type, event date, document version, last check, finding, alternative reading, and unresolved outcome. A new check date is not a complete audit. Do not import a directory's prose, photographs, counts, or judgments about associates; employment or proximity does not establish personal responsibility. A person's statement, an agency act, and a judicial finding require separate evidence.

### A developing accountability case {#developing-cases}

The [Clara Tan case study](/How-to-Hear-a-Bully/trust/clara-tan.html), prepared October 31, 2026, applies the Trust method to a developing report. It was discovered through a reader-supplied [Advocate article](https://www.advocate.com/news/people/transgender-woman-ice-new-york), then checked against original local reporting and federal regulatory text. It concerns ICE during the Trump administration. Its [source record](/How-to-Hear-a-Bully/trust/clara-tan-sources.html) distinguishes firsthand allegations, an agency response reported by journalists, direct reading of rules, and uninspected case records.

The inclusion reason is the teachable distinction between partial confirmation and unanswered treatment questions. It is not a verified-rhetoric entry, a finding of misconduct, or a representative sample. Public interest in accountability does not require publishing private medical or immigration identifiers. Alternative explanations are labeled as hypotheses, not invented official responses. Draft status and the publication hold remain in place; dates identify a bounded check, not automatic monitoring.

## What the dates mean

Dates identify particular checks, not continuous monitoring.

- **Middleman:** each entry shows its own verification information. An age warning asks for another check; it does not mean that a fresh check has happened. Confirm current requirements and fees with the agency before acting.
- **Bully:** the published guide identifies its second edition as October 2026. Its verification records and revision ledger provide more detail. That edition label does not establish an exact date when every source was last checked.
- **Crisis:** the guide and notes completed their documented editorial check on **9 October 2026**. The notes disclose remaining access limits. The teaching method has not been tested with readers.
- **Trust:** the guide and notes completed their documented editorial check on **9 October 2026**. The notes disclose remaining access limits. The five questions have not been tested with readers.

Fixing a layout, checking a link or rebuilding a page does not renew its research date. A working link alone does not prove that the information behind it is current.

## AI assistance and responsibility

These projects use AI assistance for research, drafting, critical review and development. The project owner directs the work. That does not mean every factual statement has been individually checked by a human, or that review by another AI amounts to independent expert certification. Errors can survive repeated reviews.

Sources and recorded limitations let readers examine the work. Relevant professional expertise remains valuable, especially where a decision depends on circumstances a short guide cannot assess.

## Report a correction

For Bully, Crisis, Trust or this collection, [open a GitHub issue](https://github.com/ExtiVaries/How-to-Hear-a-Bully/issues). Include the page, the passage or broken link, and a source or explanation of the problem. Posting requires a GitHub account, and issues are public. Do not include private case details, identity documents or passwords.

For Middleman, use its [corrections and feedback page](https://dont-pay-a-middleman.vercel.app/about#contact). Its existing inbox uses an external provider, and messages may be reviewed with AI assistance. Read the privacy and response limits there before sending. Neither route guarantees a reply or urgent help.
