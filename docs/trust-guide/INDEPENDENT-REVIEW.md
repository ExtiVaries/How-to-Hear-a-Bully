## Corrections before publication

### 1. Remove the unsupported inference about the library screenshot

**Passage, Exercise A:** “The screenshot was accurate when it was posted; the situation changed.”

The scenario establishes that the old library notice concerned September. It does not establish that the neighbor's current post was accurate when posted. Reproducing an old notice faithfully and using it to make an accurate current claim are different things. The exercise should teach that distinction.

**Correction:** “The old notice described the September closure; it does not establish the hours now.” Keep the next sentence, “Nothing here shows the poster meant to mislead,” and the practical stopping point. This is an error in the exercise's inference, not an unresolved historical fact.

### 2. Correct the notes' account of independent evidence

**Passage, notes introduction:** “Notes 1 and 2 share investigators, and notes 5 and 6 come from the same institution, so neither pair is two independent lines of evidence.”

Shared investigators do not establish shared observations. The first study concerns selected adults evaluating websites; the second concerns a classroom intervention. They are different studies, even though they are not replications by wholly separate research teams. Institutional branding likewise does not determine the independence of every underlying argument. Notes 5 and 6 are guidance, not two empirical validations of this guide. The blanket conclusion also conflicts with Question 3's sound distinction between shared data and independent analysis.

**Correction:** Preserve the provenance facts, but distinguish shared teams, separate studies, and professional guidance. Suggested direction: “Notes 1 and 2 share investigators but examine different groups and tasks. Notes 5 and 6 are guidance from the same institution. These sources do not jointly validate our five questions.” [2019 study abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/016146811912101102); [2022 abstract record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1372738).

### 3. Remove the unsupported duration claim from note 4

**Passage, heading:** “Brief training for adults had small, short-lived measured effects.”

This reintroduces the interpretation the note's own limitations correctly reject. An initial difference and a later statistically indistinguishable difference do not, by themselves, demonstrate a decline or disappearance. Improvement in the control group also matters.

**Correction:** Use “Brief training for adults: small and uncertain differences” or similarly neutral wording. Keep the limited follow-up finding in the main guide; there is no need to delete this counterweight merely to reduce word count. See the source-specific findings below. [Original study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976261453813).

### 4. Narrow the safety callout's deference to authority

**Passage:** “following the current instructions of whoever is responsible for safety where you are.”

The no-delay principle is sound. The qualification is too broad: holding responsibility alone does not establish relevant competence, and conflicting or unclear instructions are possible. This undercuts the guide's central lesson that trust belongs to a particular question and evidence.

**Correction:** Keep a short callout about immediate protective action and appropriate emergency help or current, relevant safety guidance. Remove “whoever is responsible.” For example: “If someone is in immediate danger, take appropriate protective action and seek emergency help. Don't delay urgent action to work through these questions.” Do not expand it into emergency-response instructions.

## Source verification and remaining limits

### Note 1 — Wineburg and McGrew, 2019

The [publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/016146811912101102) supports the sample of 45, three reported tasks, and the comparative description of fact-checkers' approach and performance. The draft correctly labels this a small selected-group comparison rather than a randomized instruction experiment. Retain it. Full text was not obtained in this review; the draft makes no unsupported effect-size claim from it.

### Note 2 — Wineburg and colleagues, 2022

**Keep the guide's bounded sentence.** The [ERIC final-article abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1372738) supports 271 intervention and 228 comparison students, six 50-minute lessons, and improved credibility assessment. The [author-project page](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/research/lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet/) calls the analysis cluster-randomized; the [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3936112) combines matched-control language with a cluster-randomized analysis description.

Matching and random assignment can coexist. This is **unresolved allocation detail, not a demonstrated contradiction or confirmed packet error**. Final-paper methods were not retrieved. The [APA supplement](https://supp.apa.org/psycarticles/supplemental/edu0000740/EDU_2020_0630_Supplemental_Materials.pdf) documents assessment materials but does not resolve allocation. Retain the final abstract's 50-minute duration. In note 2 and the handoff, replace language claiming a confirmed design conflict with a concise account of the different descriptions and the unverified allocation procedure. Do not label the study nonrandomized either.

### Note 3 — Aslett and colleagues, 2024

The [author-written abstract and figure captions on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38123685/) support the main finding and its concentration among people receiving lower-quality results. The guide's broad warning can stay. Do not imply that search-result quality was itself randomly assigned.

The earlier packet check retrieved the opening PDF pages, including the distinction between randomized studies 1/5 and before/after studies 2–4. Renewed full-text retrieval failed in this review. Consequently, the entire “roughly 6–11 percentage points” range and the “moderate” agreement characterization have **not been independently cleared here**. This is an access limit, not evidence they are wrong. Give exact primary-page/table support before retaining them, or omit those nonessential details from the publication notes. Qualify “The prompt to search was randomized” with “in studies 1 and 5.”

Keep a precise access history: the draft reports its own PDF inspection; this review does not certify that claim or pretend to have repeated every part of it. The guide need not lose its supported, qualitative finding because supplementary details remain unchecked.

### Note 4 — Oswald and colleagues, 2026

The [full publisher HTML](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976261453813) was accessible and inspected during this review, including methods and results. It confirms small initial differences, not all statistically clear, and improved control-group performance at follow-up. The current main-text sentence is defensible; “differences from the control group were no longer statistically clear” would be more precise than “no longer measurably ahead.” Avoid “no lasting effect.”

**Update the access note.** The society's login link does not mean the full paper is unavailable everywhere. Cite the accessible publisher text, distinguishing this review's inspection from the drafter's earlier abstract-only check. The methods describe different interventions, so do not imply identical training in both groups. The **527–539** page range is confirmed by the [PubMed citation record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42406504/). No effect sizes need adding to the guide.

### Note 5 — National Academies, 2019

Recommendation 7-3 supports the guide's caution about treating one contrary result as automatic refutation of a wider evidence base. Keep the distinction between that guidance and an inflexible rule against decisive new evidence. The quotation matches the [official chapter](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25303/chapter/10).

The pagination issue is resolved: the online chapter **does** display printed-page boundaries, and Recommendation 7-3 is on **p. 161**. Remove the claim that the online text does not map passages to pages. Use the verified pinpoint rather than carrying forward a page-access caveat.

### Note 6 — National Academies, 2026

The [September 2026, version 1.0 module](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/pubs/29488/versions/v1.0/modules/module-3.html) supports the attributed guidance on expertise, substantive dissent, community knowledge, and transparency about advocacy. The quoted excerpts are consistent with the retrieved text. It explicitly treats analysis and advocacy as legitimate activities requiring clarity about the role being played. It does not require scientists never to advocate. Keep the guidance/empirical-evidence distinction.

### Note 7 — ICMJE, January 2026

The [official PDF](https://www.icmje.org/icmje-recommendations.pdf) supports the passages about funder roles, corrections, evolving knowledge, and potentially invalidating errors. **Section III.A is on printed p. 8.** The extraction marks that page `P7` because its PDF page indexing begins at zero; its footer is 8. The prior packet's page number was correct. Remove the alleged discrepancy; sections II.B and III.A, with p. 8 for corrections, are sufficient pinpoint references.

One precision point: the source describes the *potential* for conflict and bias when a secondary interest may influence judgment. The note's “A conflict exists when” can be softened to “A potential conflict arises when” to preserve the source's framing. Neither perceived nor actual conflict automatically proves the claim false. The application to civic sources remains explicitly an editorial adaptation.

## Small improvements, without restructuring

1. **Question 2:** Change “expertise in one field doesn't carry over to another” to “expertise in one field doesn't **automatically** carry over to another.” Relevant methods and knowledge can cross disciplinary boundaries.
2. **Question 4:** Change “keep advocacy separate from assessment” to wording about making clear when one is assessing evidence and when one is advocating a policy. This is closer to the cited guidance and does not make advocacy a disqualification.
3. **Accountable reviews:** If a few words can be exchanged, make “accountable” concrete: a review identifies its authors and evidence and explains its conclusions. Avoid another checklist.
4. **Unmeasured frequency:** “Some conflicts shrink here” and “That may be enough” are modest alternatives to “Many conflicts” and “Often that is enough.” No new research is needed to make those small reductions in certainty.
5. **Research paragraph:** Keeping Oswald in the main text is defensible. If the paragraph feels dense, split it before “But more searching isn't automatically better.” Cut redundant qualification elsewhere before removing a consequential limit. No new study or political example is needed.

## Adversarial reader assessment

A conservative reader could reasonably wonder whether “fact-checker,” institutional guidance, and consensus are being used as shortcuts for authority. The draft substantially answers this through relevant competence, contrary evidence, earned mistrust, and the outsider illustration. Clarifying advocacy helps; adding a politically congenial source solely for balance would not.

A progressive reader could read the cautions about financial conflicts and individual mistakes as excusing institutional harm if those sentences were isolated. Their existing context recognizes patterns of unreliability and control over evidence. Keep that context and narrow the safety-authority exception.

A disengaged reader can follow both exercises without knowing any political history. “Accountable review” is the least concrete instruction; a brief explanation would help. The next-step invitation and stopping rule make the full five-question method optional for a simple decision. These are plausible reader concerns, not results of reader testing.
