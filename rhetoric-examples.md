# Rhetoric in Practice: Verified Examples

*A companion tracker to [How to Hear a Bully](how-to-hear-a-bully.md). Started October 10, 2026.*

This is a growing set of documented examples for practicing the guide's five questions. **An entry is not a verdict that the speaker is a bully or intended to deceive.** We distinguish the original wording, its context, the rhetorical mechanism, contrary evidence, and unresolved questions. We apply the same standard across parties and countries.

## March 27, 2025 — When a label is paired with the power to remove {#rubio-visa-label-2025-03-27}

**Speaker and event:** Marco Rubio, March 27, 2025 joint press availability in Georgetown, Guyana. **Evidence status:** Full State Department written transcript verified in an October 12, 2025 archive capture, alongside the official video and court record. Audio was not independently checked. **Last checked:** October 29, 2026. This is a historical rhetoric case study, not a report of today's appellate outcome.

**Why this example:** It was found through [Who He Hired's Rubio profile](https://whohehired.com/people/rubio), a directory of one administration's hires and selected because the original exchange, conduct-based defense, individual record, and court limits can be checked together. These are the inclusion criteria regardless of party. This is not a representative sample, party comparison, or judgment about other hires.

“Every time I find one of these lunatics, I take away their visa.” The [archived official written transcript](https://perma.cc/6LUR-TL7J) places this sentence after a question about reports of 300 revoked visas. After another count-confirmation question, Rubio describes people “tearing things up.” Earlier, a reporter asked about a Turkish student in the Boston area and what conduct justified revocation. The court identifies her as Rümeysa Öztürk, arrested in Somerville, Massachusetts. Rubio's answer invoked vandalism, harassment, takeovers, riots, and visa misrepresentation. The written transcript, [rendered official video transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AtHuznPn7lo), and [court record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-mad-1_25-cv-10685/pdf/USCOURTS-mad-1_25-cv-10685-3.pdf) support this order and context.

The mechanism worth examining is a disparaging category linked to an official consequence. The strongest objection to this reading is concrete: Rubio attached the label to alleged misconduct in the same exchange. It may be shorthand for that stated conduct, not a substitute for evidence. The critical question is whether the conduct was established for each affected person. This is an interpretation to test, not a measured audience effect, a diagnosis, or proof that an insult made a policy unlawful.

Protecting students from harassment and keeping buildings accessible are legitimate concerns. Harsh language does not decide legality; statutory discretion does not resolve a constitutional challenge. Check whether the category matches individual evidence and whether the same rule would apply to another viewpoint.

The individual record matters. The district court reproduces a State memorandum acknowledging that DHS supplied no evidence of Öztürk's antisemitic activity. It identifies Senior Bureau Official John Armstrong as the revocation decisionmaker, and discusses the op-ed, criticism, and claimed organizational connection. Do not infer from Rubio's first-person wording that he personally signed or decided this revocation. An absence of evidence supplied to the memo is not a universal finding about everything a person has ever done.

The district court found viewpoint-discriminatory enforcement chilling protected speech unconstitutional (p. 101), with related Administrative Procedure Act violations discussed at pp. 138–144. It rejected a separate constitutional violation under the specific threats theory before it. Those are district-court findings, not a verdict on all immigration enforcement.

Both sides appealed following the [January 22, 2026 annotated judgment](https://iptp-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/2026.01.22_Annotated_Judgement_-_AAUP_v._Rubio.pdf), ECF 313: the government in [No. 26-1141](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca1/26-1141) and plaintiffs in [No. 26-1195](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca1/26-1195). The reproduced April 7 order grants an unopposed partial stay limited to the Part IV sanction pending further order. Available docket copies cover only August 2026, not today's disposition. A limited remedy stay is not a merits reversal.

Slow down the category. Which person's conduct is at issue? What evidence supports the consequence? Does the rule concern unlawful acts, protected expression, or an association between them? Who can test the decision, and what remedy is available? Apply these questions to officials of any party.

Remove the insult and restate the rule neutrally. If consistent application and specific evidence still support the consequence, the rhetoric alone does not settle the policy question. If the case depends on the label, ask what evidence it replaces.

**A calm question:** “What specific conduct, rather than a political viewpoint or a label, makes this person subject to this action, and where can we check the rule and the evidence?”

**Limits:** This example does not decide an individual's immigration case, infer private motives, or label Rubio a bully. Attribution rests on the archived official written record, not an independent audio check. Contrary evidence may change the interpretation. The archive is an official-origin record preserved by a third party; the dockets are third-party reproductions, not complete current official coverage.

## October 8, 2026 — JD Vance defines “socialism”

**Speaker:** U.S. Vice President JD Vance. **Setting:** Press conference about alleged employment-visa abuse, during questions from reporters.

**What was asked:** A reporter asked how Vance personally defined socialism and why socialist candidates were appearing across the country.

**What he said:** Vance first acknowledged that socialism is classically associated with prioritizing workers, then said he did not know what the term meant anymore. He described what he thought it meant in the current American context: replacing American workers with foreign workers and allowing boys to participate in girls' sports. He argued that self-described socialists would oppose the administration's visa restrictions.

**Why this is an example:** This is **changing a political label's meaning**: an economic tradition associated with social ownership or control of productive resources was redefined around two positions Vance attributed to political opponents. It changes the subject from economic organization to immigration and transgender participation in sports.

**What the example does not prove:** It does not establish Vance's private beliefs, deliberate dishonesty, or bullying by itself. His broader criticism of visa practices is a separate factual and policy question. The screenshot circulating online omits his introductory acknowledgement of socialism's traditional worker-oriented associations.

**Question to ask:** *“Are you defining socialism as an economic system, or describing policies you associate with politicians who call themselves socialists?”*

**Evidence:**
- [Full press-conference transcript, October 8, 2026, around 40:21–41:31 (Roll Call Factbase)](https://rollcall.com/factbase/trump/transcript/donald-trump-press-conference-jd-vance-visa-fraud-october-8-2026/)
- [Contemporaneous video and extended quotation (RealClearPolitics)](https://www.realclearpolitics.com/video/2026/10/08/vance_i_dont_know_what_the_term_socialism_means_anymore_its_really_not_about_the_working_class.html)

## October 9, 2026 — Markwayne Mullin redirects a question about deportation aircraft

**Speaker:** U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. **Setting:** Press conference about ICE officers shooting Oscar Belgal in New York City.

**What happened:** A reporter challenged Mullin's assertion that immigration enforcement was being “ramped up” ahead of the midterms. Mullin denied any increase, saying ICE was doing its regular work. The reporter then asked whether DHS would continue using aircraft purchased under the prior administration for deportations. Mullin declined to answer, called the question an attempt to divert attention from the arrest, criticized “fake news,” and suggested the reporter was defending someone he described as dangerous.

**Why this is an example:** **Questioning the questioner's loyalties rather than addressing the question.** Asking about the use of government aircraft does not, by itself, imply support for the person arrested. The response associated an operational oversight question with a position the reporter had not expressed.

**Alternative reading and limits:** Mullin was holding a press conference about a specific shooting and could reasonably prefer questions about that incident. He did answer the enforcement-surge question with a denial; it would be inaccurate to say he refused to address both topics. The transcript does not prove his private intentions, whether the aircraft were luxury aircraft, or whether an alleged arrest-increase directive existed.

**Question to ask:** *“Can you answer the question about the aircraft without assuming that asking it means defending the person who was arrested?”*

**Evidence:**
- [CNN transcript of the press-conference exchange, October 9, 2026](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/cnc/date/2026-10-09/segment/06)
- [Reuters reporting on the press conference and ICE shooting](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/ice-officials-defend-shooting-new-york-man-attack-sanctuary-policies-2026-10-09/)

**Status:** Exchange verified from transcript; rhetorical classification is editorial analysis. **Last checked:** October 10, 2026.

## October 10, 2026 — Mike Rogers endorses a “terrorist” label in a football dispute

**Speaker:** Mike Rogers, Republican nominee for U.S. Senate in Michigan. **Setting:** A repost on X of a message from sports commentator Dave Portnoy about Rogers's Democratic opponent, Abdul El-Sayed.

**What happened:** El-Sayed, a University of Michigan graduate, joked about whether Michigan football could win without cheating. Portnoy, a Michigan fan, posted the clip and wrote, “This guy really is a terrorist.” Rogers reposted it, adding, “@StoolPresidente is exactly right.” When *The Detroit News* asked whether Rogers literally believed the allegation, his campaign spokeswoman responded with a joke about Ohio State talking points rather than a direct yes or no.

**Why this is an example:** **Amplifying a loaded label while leaving its intended meaning open.** The clip concerns a football rivalry, not evidence of terrorist activity. “Terrorist” ordinarily alleges a connection to political violence; an endorsement by a Senate candidate can be read either as sports hyperbole or as a more serious insinuation. That ambiguity is relevant because Rogers had raised separate allegations involving El-Sayed's family and terrorist organizations during their October 8 debate; contemporary reporting found no evidence that El-Sayed personally supported those groups.

**Alternative reading and limits:** Football fans routinely use exaggerated insults, and Portnoy's initial remark is plausibly a joke. The spokeswoman's response emphasized that sports context. This record does **not** establish that either man was making a literal allegation, intended to intimidate El-Sayed, or committed unlawful incitement. It also does not adjudicate every disputed claim raised during the earlier debate. The same test—does the context support the label, and will the speaker clarify its meaning?—applies regardless of party.

**Question to ask:** *“Was your agreement only football banter, or were you alleging that your opponent is actually involved in terrorism? If the latter, what evidence supports that?”*

**Evidence:**
- [Rogers's October 10 X post (primary post linked by *The Detroit News*)](https://x.com/MikeRogersForMI/status/2108924755705291160)
- [*The Detroit News*, October 10: full football context, Rogers's response, and spokeswoman's clarification (syndicated copy)](https://www.aol.com/articles/rogers-shares-portnoy-sports-post-180523000.html)
- [OutKick, October 11: independent report of the posts and El-Sayed's reply](https://www.foxnews.com/outkick-sports/abdul-el-sayed-feuds-dave-portnoy-response-michigan-cheating-allegation-comments)
- [Michigan Public Radio, October 9: report on the October 8 debate and the evidence limits of earlier allegations](https://www.wgvunews.org/news/2026-10-09/rogers-el-sayed-trade-attacks-in-contentious-michigan-senate-debate)

**Status:** Wording and sports context corroborated by contemporaneous reporting and a linked original post; direct X access was restricted during this check. Rhetorical analysis is editorial. **Last checked:** October 12, 2026.

## October 11, 2026 — Donald Trump announces an energy ceasefire

**Speaker and setting:** U.S. President Donald Trump, in a Truth Social post.

**What he said:** On October 11, 2026, Donald Trump announced an immediately effective energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine and wrote, 'Both parties have agreed.' His wording presented mutual agreement and an immediate start as settled. The public responses examined here did not establish the same certainty about the arrangement's terms and status.

**Context:** Ukraine welcomed the announcement and said it was ready to refrain from striking Russian energy infrastructure. It also sought details of the American–Russian dialogue and expected reciprocal Russian restraint. On October 12 and 13, Interfax reported that Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said contacts were continuing.

**Editorial interpretation, alternative reading and limits:** The useful distinction is between a confident announcement and what listeners can verify. These sources do not establish what private assurances Trump had received, whether he knowingly misstated anything, or whether every relevant term remained unresolved. Ukraine's welcome and stated readiness are important counterevidence to any suggestion that both parties rejected the proposal. Reports of subsequent attacks raise a separate question about scope and compliance; attacks alone would not prove that no agreement existed.

**Practical question:** What has each side confirmed, which facilities are covered, when does the agreement begin, and how will compliance be checked?

**Apply the same standard:** Apply the same questions to a leader you support. This statement alone does not establish bullying or a pattern of manipulation.

**Evidence:**

## October 2026 — Texas mailers: verify authorship before assigning blame {#tent-mailers-2026-10}

**What happened:** October texts and mailers directed Texans to the Transgender Education Network of Texas (TENT). CNN reported that the mailers used its photographs, logo and a nearly matching return address. [TENT denies authorizing or distributing the communications](https://transtexas.org/) and calls them impersonations. According to [CNN's account of TENT's court filing](https://kesq.com/news/national-world/cnn-national/2026/10/28/texas-nonprofit-says-it-was-impersonated-in-lgbtq-mailers-a-judge-cleared-the-way-to-find-the-sender/), TENT reported violent threats and suspended normal programming.

**Institutional response:** Rep. Jeff Leach [initially requested an attorney-general investigation of TENT](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/14/texas-transgender-texts-cease-desist/); a subsequent letter [sought the actual sender's identity after doubts about attribution emerged](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/22/texas-transgender-pamphlet-da-investigation/). That request does not establish that the attorney general opened an investigation. Separately, District Attorney Sarah Stogner opened a potential-forgery investigation into the mailers.

**Why this example:** Discovered through a reader-supplied CNN report, it illustrates a question about authorship: a genuine logo, photograph or web link does not establish who authorized a message. Verify that connection before treating the communication as evidence against the organization named in it. See the [Trust guide's source questions](/How-to-Hear-a-Bully/trust/#how-could-each-source-know).

**Alternative reading:** Texas Transgender Student Union LLC, the Wyoming entity associated with the mailers, [argues that the communications discuss public resources and are protected anonymous speech](https://www.aol.com/articles/wyoming-llc-hires-paxton-impeachment-171141000.html). Citing someone else's work can be legitimate. Its defense and TENT's denial require examination of the actual presentation and authorization; neither settles every legal question.

**Accountability and limits:** [Reporting describes an October 27 ruling authorizing pre-suit discovery, followed by an appeal](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/28/texas-transgender-mailer-text-court-order-deposition/). Responsibility and liability remain unresolved. The reviewed evidence does not establish the ultimate sponsor, funding source or electoral purpose. [KUT reported scheduled questioning](https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-10-28/transgender-texts-mail-court-hearing-austin), while [KXAN reported a pause following the appeal](https://www.aol.com/articles/wyoming-llc-hires-paxton-impeachment-171141000.html). The operative timetable and any resulting public disclosure remain unconfirmed in this check.

**A calm question:** “What establishes who authorized and sent this material, and what was checked before treating it as evidence against the organization named in it?”

**Repair to check:** Do corrections reach the original audience, and can disrupted services resume safely?

**Evidence status:** TENT's statement was read directly; court actions and harms are attributed to reporting. Underlying orders were not independently inspected. Interpretation is editorial. **Last checked:** October 29, 2026.

### October 30–31, 2026 — Court stay and attribution update

**October 30 update — disclosure paused:** The [Texas Tribune’s October 30 report](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/30/texas-trans-mailers-supreme-court-block-deposition/) says the Texas Supreme Court temporarily blocked questioning of Texas Transgender Student Union LLC. The court gave no explanation. TENT’s response is due November 2 at 10 a.m.; this is a filing deadline, not a promised disclosure or decision date. The stay does not determine whether the mailers were lawful.

**Keep the attribution separate:** An earlier deposition identified the Republican Women’s Caucus Fund and an individual named John Braun in connection with the texts. The fund and Washington state Sen. John Braun deny involvement. TENT’s lawyer suspects a common source for texts and mailers but acknowledges no evidence yet linking them. Neither the printed mailers’ sponsorship nor individual lawmakers’ involvement is established by this report.

**Possible procedural reasons — not findings:** A pause can preserve meaningful review before identities are irreversibly disclosed, allow responses, or permit examination of the requirements and scope of pre-suit questioning. [Texas Rule 202](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462349/texas-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf) requires specified findings for such depositions; [appellate Rule 52.10](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1463134/texas-rules-of-appellate-procedure-02062026.pdf) permits temporary relief. Anonymity claims may also need examination. These are possible explanations, not the court’s stated reasons or a conclusion that the lower judge erred.

**Effect and motive:** The delay could leave voters without answers before November 3. That consequence does not establish a political motive. TENT’s lawyer says it can sue some responsible parties, and the Tribune reports a separate forgery investigation. A stay, an investigation, an allegation and a finding of liability are different events.

**Question to follow:** What does the next order actually decide, what evidence identifies each sender, and what remains undisclosed? **Update checked:** October 31, 2026. Court developments are attributed to reporting; the full case-specific order and emergency motion were not independently inspected. This dated update supplements the October 29 record above.

## How new examples are added

Include the original date and speaker, an accessible primary recording or transcript where possible, the full relevant context, the exact claim (short quotations only), the best-supported classification, an alternative reading, the evidence limits, a calm question readers can use, and a last-checked date. If a claim cannot be verified, do not present it as a confirmed example. Prefer a new entry to silently rewriting an older record; document substantive corrections.
