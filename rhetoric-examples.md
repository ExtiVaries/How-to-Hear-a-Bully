# Rhetoric in Practice: Verified Examples

*A companion tracker to [How to Hear a Bully](how-to-hear-a-bully.md). Started October 10, 2026.*

This is a growing set of documented examples for practicing the guide's five questions. **An entry is not a verdict that the speaker is a bully or intended to deceive.** We distinguish the original wording, its context, the rhetorical mechanism, contrary evidence, and unresolved questions. We apply the same standard across parties and countries.

## October 8, 2026 — JD Vance defines “socialism”

**Speaker:** U.S. Vice President JD Vance. **Setting:** Press conference about alleged employment-visa abuse, during questions from reporters.

**What was asked:** A reporter asked how Vance personally defined socialism and why socialist candidates were appearing across the country.

**What he said:** Vance first acknowledged that socialism is classically associated with prioritizing workers, then said he did not know what the term meant anymore. He described what he thought it meant in the current American context: replacing American workers with foreign workers and allowing boys to participate in girls' sports. He argued that self-described socialists would oppose the administration's visa restrictions.

**Why this is an example:** This is **changing a political label's meaning**: an economic tradition associated with social ownership or control of productive resources was redefined around two positions Vance attributed to political opponents. It changes the subject from economic organization to immigration and transgender participation in sports.

**What the example does not prove:** It does not establish Vance's private beliefs, deliberate dishonesty, or bullying by itself. His broader criticism of visa practices is a separate factual and policy question. The screenshot circulating online omits his introductory acknowledgement of socialism's traditional worker-oriented associations.

**Question to ask:** *“Are you defining socialism as an economic system, or describing policies you associate with politicians who call themselves socialists?”*

## How new examples are added

Include the original date and speaker, an accessible primary recording or transcript where possible, the full relevant context, the exact claim (short quotations only), the best-supported classification, an alternative reading, the evidence limits, a calm question readers can use, and a last-checked date. If a claim cannot be verified, do not present it as a confirmed example. Prefer a new entry to silently rewriting an older record; document substantive corrections.
