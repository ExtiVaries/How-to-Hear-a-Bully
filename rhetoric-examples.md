# Rhetoric in Practice: Verified Examples

*A companion tracker to [How to Hear a Bully](how-to-hear-a-bully.md). Started October 10, 2026.*

This is a growing set of documented examples for practicing the guide's five questions. **An entry is not a verdict that the speaker is a bully or intended to deceive.** We distinguish the original wording, its context, the rhetorical mechanism, contrary evidence, and unresolved questions. We apply the same standard across parties and countries.

## October 8, 2026 — JD Vance defines “socialism”

**Speaker:** U.S. Vice President JD Vance. **Setting:** Press conference about alleged employment-visa abuse, during questions from reporters.

**What was asked:** A reporter asked how Vance personally defined socialism and why socialist candidates were appearing across the country.

**What he said:** Vance first acknowledged that socialism is classically associated with prioritizing workers, then said he did not know what the term meant anymore. He described what he thought it meant in the current American context: replacing American workers with foreign workers and allowing boys to participate in girls' sports. He argued that self-described socialists would oppose the administration's visa restrictions.

**Why this is an example:** This is **changing a political label's meaning**: an economic tradition associated with social ownership or control of productive resources was redefined around two positions Vance attributed to political opponents. It changes the subject from economic organization to immigration and transgender participation in sports.

**What the example does not prove:** It does not establish Vance's private beliefs, deliberate dishonesty, or bullying by itself. His broader criticism of visa practices is a separate factual and policy question. The screenshot circulating online omits his introductory acknowledgement of socialism's traditional worker-oriented associations.

**Question to ask:** *“Are you defining socialism as an economic system, or describing policies you associate with politicians who call themselves socialists?”*

## October 9, 2026 — Markwayne Mullin redirects a question about deportation aircraft

**Speaker:** U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. **Setting:** Press conference about ICE officers shooting Oscar Belgal in New York City.

**What happened:** A reporter challenged Mullin's assertion that immigration enforcement was being “ramped up” ahead of the midterms. Mullin denied any increase, saying ICE was doing its regular work. The reporter then asked whether DHS would continue using aircraft purchased under the prior administration for deportations. Mullin declined to answer, called the question an attempt to divert attention from the arrest, criticized “fake news,” and suggested the reporter was defending someone he described as dangerous.

**Why this is an example:** **Questioning the questioner's loyalties rather than addressing the question.** Asking about the use of government aircraft does not, by itself, imply support for the person arrested. The response associated an operational oversight question with a position the reporter had not expressed.

**Alternative reading and limits:** Mullin was holding a press conference about a specific shooting and could reasonably prefer questions about that incident. He did answer the enforcement-surge question with a denial; it would be inaccurate to say he refused to address both topics. The transcript does not prove his private intentions, whether the aircraft were luxury aircraft, or whether an alleged arrest-increase directive existed.

**Question to ask:** *“Can you answer the question about the aircraft without assuming that asking it means defending the person who was arrested?”*

- [Reuters reporting on the press conference and ICE shooting](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/ice-officials-defend-shooting-new-york-man-attack-sanctuary-policies-2026-10-09/)

## October 10, 2026 — Mike Rogers endorses a “terrorist” label in a football dispute

**Speaker:** Mike Rogers, Republican nominee for U.S. Senate in Michigan. **Setting:** A repost on X of a message from sports commentator Dave Portnoy about Rogers's Democratic opponent, Abdul El-Sayed.

**What happened:** El-Sayed, a University of Michigan graduate, joked about whether Michigan football could win without cheating. Portnoy, a Michigan fan, posted the clip and wrote, “This guy really is a terrorist.” Rogers reposted it, adding, “@StoolPresidente is exactly right.” When *The Detroit News* asked whether Rogers literally believed the allegation, his campaign spokeswoman responded with a joke about Ohio State talking points rather than a direct yes or no.

**Why this is an example:** **Amplifying a loaded label while leaving its intended meaning open.** The clip concerns a football rivalry, not evidence of terrorist activity. “Terrorist” ordinarily alleges a connection to political violence; an endorsement by a Senate candidate can be read either as sports hyperbole or as a more serious insinuation. That ambiguity is relevant because Rogers had raised separate allegations involving El-Sayed's family and terrorist organizations during their October 8 debate; contemporary reporting found no evidence that El-Sayed personally supported those groups.

**Alternative reading and limits:** Football fans routinely use exaggerated insults, and Portnoy's initial remark is plausibly a joke. The spokeswoman's response emphasized that sports context. This record does **not** establish that either man was making a literal allegation, intended to intimidate El-Sayed, or committed unlawful incitement. It also does not adjudicate every disputed claim raised during the earlier debate. The same test—does the context support the label, and will the speaker clarify its meaning?—applies regardless of party.

**Question to ask:** *“Was your agreement only football banter, or were you alleging that your opponent is actually involved in terrorism? If the latter, what evidence supports that?”*

**Evidence:**

- [*The Detroit News*, October 10: full football context, Rogers's response, and spokeswoman's clarification (syndicated copy)](https://www.aol.com/articles/rogers-shares-portnoy-sports-post-180523000.html)
- [OutKick, October 11: independent report of the posts and El-Sayed's reply](https://www.foxnews.com/outkick-sports/abdul-el-sayed-feuds-dave-portnoy-response-michigan-cheating-allegation-comments)

## October 11, 2026 — Donald Trump announces an energy ceasefire

**Speaker and setting:** U.S. President Donald Trump, in a Truth Social post.

**What he said:** On October 11, 2026, Donald Trump announced an immediately effective energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine and wrote, 'Both parties have agreed.' His wording presented mutual agreement and an immediate start as settled. The public responses examined here did not establish the same certainty about the arrangement's terms and status.

**Context:** Ukraine welcomed the announcement and said it was ready to refrain from striking Russian energy infrastructure. It also sought details of the American–Russian dialogue and expected reciprocal Russian restraint. On October 12 and 13, Interfax reported that Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said contacts were continuing.

**Editorial interpretation, alternative reading and limits:** The useful distinction is between a confident announcement and what listeners can verify. These sources do not establish what private assurances Trump had received, whether he knowingly misstated anything, or whether every relevant term remained unresolved. Ukraine's welcome and stated readiness are important counterevidence to any suggestion that both parties rejected the proposal. Reports of subsequent attacks raise a separate question about scope and compliance; attacks alone would not prove that no agreement existed.

**Practical question:** What has each side confirmed, which facilities are covered, when does the agreement begin, and how will compliance be checked?

**Apply the same standard:** Apply the same questions to a leader you support. This statement alone does not establish bullying or a pattern of manipulation.

## How new examples are added

Include the original date and speaker, an accessible primary recording or transcript where possible, the full relevant context, the exact claim (short quotations only), the best-supported classification, an alternative reading, the evidence limits, a calm question readers can use, and a last-checked date. If a claim cannot be verified, do not present it as a confirmed example. Prefer a new entry to silently rewriting an older record; document substantive corrections.
